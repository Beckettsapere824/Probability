# M1.5 Bayes’ Theorem / 贝叶斯公式

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.3 Conditional Probability](M1.3.md), [M1.4 Law of Total Probability](M1.4.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3; medical diagnosis, committed-student assignment submission, base-rate fallacy; odds form.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

You observe data $B$ (positive test, submitted homework, signal) and want the updated probability of a hidden cause $A_{i}$ (disease, committed student, true state). The model usually gives $\Pr(B\mid A_{i})$ easily; Bayes turns that into $\Pr(A_{i}\mid B)$ using the prior and the law of total probability.

观测到数据 $B$（阳性、交作业、信号），想更新隐藏原因 $A_{i}$（患病、用功学生、真实状态）的概率。模型常容易给出 $\Pr(B\mid A_{i})$；贝叶斯用先验与全概率把它翻成 $\Pr(A_{i}\mid B)$。

---

## Step 1: Derive Bayes from definitions / 第一步：从定义推出

By definition $\Pr(A_{i}\mid B)=\Pr(A_{i}\cap B)/\Pr(B)$ and $\Pr(A_{i}\cap B)=\Pr(B\mid A_{i})\Pr(A_{i})$.  
If $\{A_{j}\}$ partition the sample space,

$$\Pr(B)=\sum_{j}\Pr(B\mid A_{j})\Pr(A_{j})$$

by total probability ([M1.4](M1.4.md)). Therefore

$$\Pr(A_{i}\mid B)=\frac{\Pr(B\mid A_{i})\Pr(A_{i})}{\sum_{j}\Pr(B\mid A_{j})\Pr(A_{j})}.$$

Numerator = prior × likelihood. Denominator = marginal probability of the data.

分子 = 先验 × 似然；分母 = 数据的边际概率。

---

## Step 2: Base rates are not optional / 第二步：基础率不可省略

Same likelihoods with a tiny prior still give a modest posterior. Ignoring $\Pr(A_{i})$ is the **base-rate fallacy**.

**Classic disease numbers.**  
$\Pr(D)=0.01$, $\Pr(+\mid D)=0.95$, $\Pr(+\mid D^{c})=0.10$.

$$\Pr(+)=0.95\cdot 0.01+0.10\cdot 0.99=0.1085,$$

$$\Pr(D\mid +)=\frac{0.95\cdot 0.01}{0.1085}\approx 0.0876.$$

Only about 9% of positive tests are true disease. The test is “95% accurate” in the sensitivity sense, yet most positives are false when the disease is rare.

同样似然、极小先验 → 后验仍可能不高。忽略先验即**基础率谬误**。疾病例子中阳性预测值约 9%。

---

## Step 3: Odds form (optional but useful) / 第三步：优势形式

$$\frac{\Pr(A\mid B)}{\Pr(A^{c}\mid B)}=\frac{\Pr(B\mid A)}{\Pr(B\mid A^{c})}\cdot\frac{\Pr(A)}{\Pr(A^{c})}.$$

Posterior odds = likelihood ratio × prior odds. Useful when comparing two hypotheses without enumerating a long partition.

后验优势 = 似然比 × 先验优势。

---

## Step 4: Sequential updates (exam flavour) / 第四步：序贯更新

Jasmine submits 3 assignments. Prior $\Pr(\text{committed})=0.7$; $\Pr(\text{submit}\mid\text{committed})=0.95$; $\Pr(\text{submit}\mid\text{not})=0.60$; independent across assignments given type.

After three submissions, posterior odds multiply by $(0.95/0.60)^{3}$. Then the predictive probability she submits the next one is

$$\Pr(\text{submit}_{4}\mid\text{data})=0.95\,\Pr(\text{committed}\mid\text{data})+0.60\,\Pr(\text{not}\mid\text{data}).$$

Past papers ask this predictive probability numerically (≈0.92 under those parameters).

给定类型下作业独立；三次提交后后验优势乘 $(0.95/0.60)^{3}$，再算下一次提交的预测概率。

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Bayes is definition + total probability — not a new axiom.  
2. Prior (base rate) always enters; “accurate test” ≠ high $\Pr(D\mid +)$.  
3. Likelihood $\Pr(B\mid A)$ is not the posterior $\Pr(A\mid B)$.  
4. Sequential independent observations multiply likelihood ratios.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Answering $\Pr(B\mid A)$ when asked $\Pr(A\mid B)$ | Read the conditioning direction |
| Dropping the prior | Base-rate fallacy |
| Denominator not equal to total $\Pr(B)$ | Sum over the full partition |
| Treating dependent tests as independent | Check the model |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** $\Pr(D)=0.02$, $\Pr(+\mid D)=0.9$, $\Pr(+\mid D^{c})=0.05$. Find $\Pr(D\mid +)$.

**Solution.**  
$\Pr(+)=0.9\cdot 0.02+0.05\cdot 0.98=0.067$.  
$\Pr(D\mid +)=0.018/0.067\approx 0.2687$.

## Practice / 练习

1. Prior $\Pr(D)=0.01$, sensitivity 0.95, false-positive rate 0.10. $\Pr(D\mid +)$?

2. In odds form, if prior odds are 1:99 and likelihood ratio is 9, what are posterior odds?

3. Why can a “99% accurate” test still yield $\Pr(D\mid +)<1/2$?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $\Pr(+)=0.95\cdot 0.01+0.10\cdot 0.99=0.1085$; posterior ≈0.0876.

2. Posterior odds $=9\times 1/99=9/99=1/11$.

3. If base rate is tiny, most positives are still false positives.

</details>
