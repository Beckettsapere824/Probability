# M3.2 LOTUS — Expectation of a Function of X / 随机变量函数的期望

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M3.1 Expectation](M3.1.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A discrete/continuous $E(g(X))$; variance via $E(X^{2})$; MGF as $E(e^{tX})$.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

You need $E(X^{2})$, $E(e^{tX})$, or $E(g(X))$ for a nonlinear $g$. Finding the full law of $Y=g(X)$ first is often harder than necessary. **LOTUS** (Law of the Unconscious Statistician) says: stay on the original distribution of $X$ and average $g(x)$ directly.

你需要 $E(X^{2})$、$E(e^{tX})$ 或一般非线性 $E(g(X))$。先求 $Y=g(X)$ 的全部分布往往更难。**LOTUS**说：留在 $X$ 的原分布上，直接对 $g(x)$ 取平均。

---

## Step 1: Discrete and continuous formulas / 第一步：离散与连续公式

**Discrete.** If $X$ has PMF $p$,

$$E\bigl(g(X)\bigr)=\sum_{x} g(x)\,p(x)$$

when $\sum |g(x)|p(x)<\infty$.

**Continuous.** If $X$ has pdf $f$,

$$E\bigl(g(X)\bigr)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x)\,f(x)\,dx$$

when the integral converges absolutely.

You do **not** need the PMF/pdf of $g(X)$ to compute its expectation.

不必先求 $g(X)$ 的分布，就能算它的期望。

---

## Step 2: Why it is “unconscious” / 第二步：为何叫「无意识」

A careful proof uses the definition of expectation for the push-forward measure of $g(X)$. In practice one writes $\sum g(x)p(x)$ without re-deriving that measure — hence “unconscious”. For this course, treat the formula as a theorem you may use freely once absolute convergence is clear.

严格证明要用 $g(X)$ 的像测度；课程中在绝对收敛下直接使用公式即可。

---

## Step 3: When not to bother with LOTUS / 第三步：何时不必用 LOTUS

If $g(x)=ax+b$ is linear, use linearity of expectation:

$$E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b.$$

No sum/integral expansion needed. LOTUS is for **nonlinear** $g$ (squares, exponentials, indicators of complicated sets, etc.).

线性函数直接用线性性；LOTUS 用于**非线性** $g$。

---

## Step 4: Workhorse applications / 第四步：主力应用

1. **Variance:** $\mathrm{Var}(X)=E(X^{2})-[E(X)]^{2}$ needs $E(X^{2})$ via LOTUS.  
2. **MGF:** $M_X(t)=E(e^{tX})=\sum e^{tx}p(x)$ or $\int e^{tx}f(x)\,dx$.  
3. **Indicators of transformed events:** $E\bigl(1_{\{g(X)\in A\}}\bigr)=\Pr(g(X)\in A)$.

---

## Step 5: Hand examples / 第五步：手算

**Discrete.** $X\in\{-1,0,1\}$ each with probability $1/3$.  
$E(X)=0$, $E(X^{2})=1\cdot\tfrac13+0+1\cdot\tfrac13=\tfrac23$.

**Another.** $p(-2)=1/4$, $p(0)=1/2$, $p(2)=1/4$.  
$E(X^{2})=4\cdot\tfrac14+0+4\cdot\tfrac14=2$, so $E(3X^{2}-1)=3\cdot 2-1=5$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. $E(g(X))$ averages $g$ under the law of $X$ — no need for the law of $g(X)$.  
2. Absolute convergence matters; otherwise “expectation” may not exist.  
3. Linear → linearity; nonlinear → LOTUS.  
4. Variance and MGF are LOTUS in disguise.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Deriving the full law of $g(X)$ when only $E(g(X))$ was asked | Use LOTUS |
| Writing $\sum g(x)$ without $p(x)$ | Weights are probabilities |
| Applying LOTUS to a divergent sum/integral | Check absolute convergence |
| Using $\sum$ for continuous $X$ | Switch to $\int g(x)f(x)\,dx$ |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** $X$ takes $-2,0,2$ with probabilities $1/4,1/2,1/4$. Find $E(X^{2})$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X)$.

**Solution.** $E(X)=0$ by symmetry. $E(X^{2})=2$. $\mathrm{Var}(X)=2-0=2$.
