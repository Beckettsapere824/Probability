# M1.7 Mutually Exclusive, Exhaustive, Equiprobable — Compatibility / 互斥、穷尽、等可能及其相容性

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.2](M1.2.md), [M1.6](M1.6.md)

**Source anchors**: partition = exclusive + exhaustive; independence vs exclusivity.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Exam language mixes “mutually exclusive”, “exhaustive”, “equally likely”, and “independent”. Some pairs of properties can hold together; others almost never do for events of positive probability.

考题混用「互斥」「穷尽」「等可能」「独立」。有的组合可以同时成立，有的在正概率事件上几乎不可能。

---

## Step 1: Definitions / 第一步：定义

- **Mutually exclusive (disjoint):** $\Pr(A\cap B)=0$ (or $A\cap B=\emptyset$).  
- **Exhaustive:** $A\cup B=\Omega$ (or a family whose union is $\Omega$).  
- **Partition:** pairwise exclusive and exhaustive.  
- **Equiprobable:** $\Pr(A)=\Pr(B)$ (for a listed family of outcomes or events).  
- **Independent:** $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$.

---

## Step 2: Compatibility table / 第二步：相容性

| Pair | Can both hold with $\Pr(A),\Pr(B)>0$? |
|------|--------------------------------------|
| Exclusive + independent | **No** — would need $\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=0$ |
| Exclusive + exhaustive | **Yes** — classical partition (e.g. $A$ and $A^{c}$) |
| Independent + exhaustive | **Sometimes** — solve $\Pr(A)+\Pr(B)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=1$ |
| Equiprobable + exclusive | **Yes** — e.g. two faces of a fair die |

---

## Step 3: Why exclusive ⊥ independent / 第三步：为何互斥与独立冲突

If exclusive and independent: $0=\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$.  
If both probabilities are positive, contradiction.  
Intuition: exclusive events “block” each other; independent events “ignore” each other.

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

$A$ and $A^{c}$: exclusive and exhaustive; independent only if $\Pr(A)\in\{0,1\}$.

Independent with $\Pr(A)=\Pr(B)=p$ and exhaustive: $2p-p^{2}=1\Rightarrow p=1$ (double root), so only degenerate cases for two events of equal probability.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Partition = exclusive + exhaustive — the setting for total probability.  
2. Positive-probability exclusive events cannot be independent.  
3. Equiprobable is about equal numbers, not about independence.  
4. Always translate verbal claims into $\Pr$ equations before concluding.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| “Independent means cannot happen together” | That is exclusivity, the opposite intuition |
| Assuming exclusive ⇒ independent | False for positive probabilities |
| Forgetting exhaustiveness when building a partition | Union must be $\Omega$ |
| Treating equiprobable outcomes as automatically independent events | Different notions |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** Two events with $\Pr(A),\Pr(B)>0$ that are mutually exclusive

A. must be independent  
B. cannot be independent  
C. must be exhaustive  
D. must be equiprobable

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Independence would require $\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=0$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** $A$ and $A^{c}$ are always

A. independent  
B. exclusive and exhaustive  
C. equiprobable  
D. empty

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition of complement: disjoint and union is $\Omega$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** A partition of $\Omega$ is a family that is

A. independent  
B. pairwise exclusive and exhaustive  
C. equiprobable  
D. infinite

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard definition used in total probability.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** Independent events $A,B$ with $\Pr(A\cup B)=1$ satisfy

A. $\Pr(A)+\Pr(B)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=1$  
B. $\Pr(A\cap B)=1$  
C. $\Pr(A)=0$  
D. exclusive

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

IE under independence.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**5.** Fair die: $A=\{1,2\}$, $B=\{3,4\}$. These are

A. exclusive and equiprobable  
B. independent  
C. exhaustive  
D. $\Pr(A\cap B)=1/6$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Disjoint, each probability $1/3$. Not exhaustive (5,6 missing).  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**6.** If $A$ and $B$ are exclusive and exhaustive, then

A. $\Pr(B)=1-\Pr(A)$  
B. $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$  
C. $\Pr(A)=\Pr(B)$  
D. $\Pr(A)=1/2$ always

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$B=A^{c}$ in the two-event case.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**7.** “Cannot both occur” is the language of

A. independence  
B. mutual exclusivity  
C. exhaustiveness  
D. equiprobability

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Verbal form of empty intersection.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** Positive-probability independent events

A. must be exclusive  
B. must satisfy $\Pr(A\cap B)>0$  
C. must be exhaustive  
D. must have $\Pr=1/2$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)>0$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
