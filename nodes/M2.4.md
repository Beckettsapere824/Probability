# M2.4 Combinations Without Replacement / 无放回组合

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M2.1](M2.1.md), [M2.3](M2.3.md)

**Source anchors**: $\binom{n}{k}$; teams; poker hands; committees.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Unordered selections of $k$ distinct items from $n$: committees, hands of cards, subsets. Order of selection does not create a new outcome.

从 $n$ 个中取 $k$ 个不同对象的**无序**选取：委员会、牌型、子集。

---

## Step 1: Formula / 第一步：公式

$$\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}=\frac{P(n,k)}{k!}.$$

Symmetry: $\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}$. Edge: $\binom{n}{0}=\binom{n}{n}=1$.

---

## Step 2: When to use / 第二步：何时使用

- Hand of 5 cards from 52 → $\binom{52}{5}$.  
- Choose 3 members from 10 for a committee (roles identical) → $\binom{10}{3}$.  
- If roles differ (chair, secretary, …) → use $P$ instead.

---

## Step 3: Hand checks / 第三步：手算

$\binom{10}{3}=120$.  
$\binom{5}{2}=10$.  
$\binom{n}{1}=n$, $\binom{n}{2}=n(n-1)/2$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Unordered + no replacement → binomial coefficient.  
2. Divide $P(n,k)$ by $k!$ because order is ignored.  
3. Symmetry $\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}$ (choose the complement).  
4. Classical probability numerators/denominators often both binomial.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using $\binom{n}{k}$ when order matters | Use $P(n,k)$ |
| Using $n^{k}$ for hands | Hands are unordered, no replacement |
| Forgetting symmetry to simplify | $\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}$ |

---

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** $\binom{10}{3}=$

A. $30$  
B. $120$  
C. $720$  
D. $1000$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$10\cdot9\cdot8/(3\cdot2\cdot1)=120$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** A 5-card hand from 52 is counted by

A. $52^{5}$  
B. $P(52,5)$  
C. $\binom{52}{5}$  
D. $5!$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Unordered, no replacement.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**3.** $\binom{n}{k}=$

A. $n^{k}$  
B. $P(n,k)/k!$  
C. $P(n,k)\cdot k!$  
D. $n! / k$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** $\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}$ is true because

A. order matters  
B. choosing $k$ is the same as leaving out $n-k$  
C. $k=n$ always  
D. replacement is allowed

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Complement count.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** $\binom{5}{2}=$

A. $10$  
B. $20$  
C. $25$  
D. $5$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$5\cdot4/2=10$.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**6.** Committee of 3 from 10 with identical roles:

A. $P(10,3)$  
B. $\binom{10}{3}$  
C. $10^{3}$  
D. $3^{10}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Unordered.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** $\binom{n}{0}=$

A. $0$  
B. $1$  
C. $n$  
D. undefined

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

One way to choose nothing.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** $\binom{n}{2}=$

A. $n$  
B. $n(n-1)$  
C. $n(n-1)/2$  
D. $n^{2}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard identity.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>
