# M4.7 Hypergeometric — Sampling Without Replacement / 超几何：不放回抽样

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.3 Combinations](M0.3.md), [M4.2 Binomial](M4.2.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A finite population sampling; exam urn problems, joint $(X,Y)$ red/white counts, $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$ negative dependence.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

An urn has a **finite** population: $K$ “success” items and $N-K$ others. You draw $n$ items **without** replacement. How many successes land in the sample?

Draws are **dependent** (each draw changes the remaining composition), so the binomial model (independent trials with fixed $p$) is wrong. The correct count distribution is **hypergeometric**.

瓮中总体**有限**：$K$ 个成功物品、$N-K$ 个其他。不放回抽 $n$ 个，样本中成功数是多少？

各次抽取**相依**（每次改变剩余构成），二项模型（固定 $p$ 的独立试验）不适用。正确的是**超几何**。

---

## Step 1: PMF from counting / 第一步：从计数写出 PMF

$$\Pr(X=k)=\frac{\binom{K}{k}\binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}},$$

for integers $k$ such that all three binomial coefficients are defined (i.e. $k$ between $\max(0,n-(N-K))$ and $\min(n,K)$); otherwise the probability is 0.

**Why this formula?**  
- Denominator: number of ways to choose any $n$-subset from $N$.  
- Numerator: choose $k$ successes from $K$ and $n-k$ failures from $N-K$.

Every $n$-subset is equally likely under uniform sampling without replacement, so the ratio is the probability.

**为何如此：** 分母是所有 $n$-子集数；分子是「$k$ 个成功 + $n-k$ 个失败」的子集数。均匀不放回下每个子集等可能。

---

## Step 2: Mean and variance / 第二步：均值与方差

$$E(X)=n\cdot\frac{K}{N}=np,\qquad p:=\frac{K}{N}.$$

(Same mean as a binomial with that $p$ — linearity of indicators still works: label items, let $I_{j}$ indicate that item $j$ is in the sample.)

$$\mathrm{Var}(X)=n\cdot\frac{K}{N}\cdot\frac{N-K}{N}\cdot\frac{N-n}{N-1}=np(1-p)\cdot\frac{N-n}{N-1}.$$

The factor $(N-n)/(N-1)$ is the **finite-population correction**. When $n\ll N$ it is ≈1 and hypergeometric ≈ binomial. When $n$ is a non-negligible fraction of $N$, variance is **smaller** than binomial (sampling without replacement is less noisy).

有限总体修正 $(N-n)/(N-1)$：当 $n\ll N$ 时≈1，超几何≈二项；当 $n$ 相对 $N$ 不可忽略时，方差**小于**二项。

---

## Step 3: Contrast table / 第三步：对比表

| | Hypergeometric | Binomial |
|--|----------------|----------|
| Population | finite $N$ | infinite / with replacement |
| Dependence | draws dependent | independent trials |
| PMF | ratio of combinations | $p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}$ times binomial coeff |
| Variance | $np(1-p)\frac{N-n}{N-1}$ | $np(1-p)$ |
| When ≈ | $n\ll N$ | — |

---

## Step 4: Joint counts and negative dependence (exam favourite) / 第四步：联合计数与负相关

Urn with 3 colours: $K_{1}$ red, $K_{2}$ white, $K_{3}$ blue, $N=K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}$. Draw $n$. Let $X=$ number of red, $Y=$ number of white. Then

$$\Pr(X=x,Y=y)=\frac{\binom{K_{1}}{x}\binom{K_{2}}{y}\binom{K_{3}}{n-x-y}}{\binom{N}{n}}.$$

Marginals are hypergeometric (red vs non-red, etc.).  
$\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)<0$ typically: more red in the sample leaves fewer slots for white (fixed sample size). Past papers ask numerical $\mathrm{Cov}$ and correlation from the joint table.

多色瓮、固定样本量 → 各类计数负相关。试卷常算 $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$。

---

## Step 5: Hand example / 第五步：手算

Urn: 5 red, 7 blue, draw 3.  
$\Pr(\text{exactly 2 red})=\dfrac{\binom{5}{2}\binom{7}{1}}{\binom{12}{3}}=\dfrac{10\cdot 7}{220}=\dfrac{7}{22}$.

Check support: cannot have $k=4$ reds when only 5 exist and $n=3$ limits further — formula automatically gives 0 if $\binom{5}{4}$ is paired with impossible $\binom{7}{-1}$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Hypergeometric = uniform $n$-subset counting.  
2. Same mean as binomial with $p=K/N$; **smaller** variance when $n$ is large relative to $N$.  
3. Without-replacement dependence → negative covariance between counts of two types in a fixed-size sample.  
4. When $N$ is huge, switch to binomial for computation.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using $\mathrm{Bin}(n,K/N)$ when $n/N$ is not small | Variance wrong; use hypergeometric |
| Allowing $k$ outside feasible range | Check $\max(0,n-(N-K))\le k\le\min(n,K)$ |
| Writing ordered-sample probabilities without adjusting | Stick to combination ratio (unordered) or divide both num and den by the same $n!$ |
| Forgetting finite-population factor in variance | $\frac{N-n}{N-1}$ |

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Urn has 3 red, 4 white, 5 blue ($N=12$). Draw 3 without replacement. Let $X=$ number of red, $Y=$ number of white.

(a) $\Pr(X=1,Y=1)$.  
(b) Marginal of $X$.  
(c) Why is $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$ expected to be negative?

**Solution.**

(a) $\dfrac{\binom{3}{1}\binom{4}{1}\binom{5}{1}}{\binom{12}{3}}=\dfrac{3\cdot 4\cdot 5}{220}=\dfrac{60}{220}=\dfrac{3}{11}$.

(b) $X\sim\mathrm{Hypergeometric}(N=12,K=3,n=3)$: $\Pr(X=k)=\dfrac{\binom{3}{k}\binom{9}{3-k}}{\binom{12}{3}}$.

(c) Fixed sample size 3: more reds leave fewer seats for whites → negative dependence.

2. Same urn. $E(X)$ if $X=$ number of white in the sample?

3. When is hypergeometric variance approximately equal to binomial variance $np(1-p)$?

2. $E(X)=3\cdot 4/10=1.2$.

3. When $n\ll N$, so that $(N-n)/(N-1)\approx 1$.
