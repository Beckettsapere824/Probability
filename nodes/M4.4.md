# M4.4 Geometric Distribution and Memorylessness / 几何分布与无记忆性

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.6 Independence](M1.6.md), [M4.1 Bernoulli](M4.1.md), [M1.3 Conditional](M1.3.md)

**Source anchors / 课件锚点**: SDST2601A discrete waiting times; exam items on Geo support, memoryless conditioning, min of independent Geos.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

You keep tossing a coin (or testing devices, or waiting for the next bus passenger of type A) until the **first** success. How many trials do you need? What is the chance you still need more than 10 trials after already failing 5 times?

That waiting time is the **geometric distribution**. Its single most important property is **memorylessness**: the past failures do not change the future waiting time. This is the discrete twin of the continuous exponential, and it appears constantly in HKU-style questions (conditional probabilities on the tail, “filled before”, min of several geometrics).

你不断投币（或检测器件、或等某类乘客）直到**第一次**成功。需要多少次试验？已经失败 5 次之后，还要再等超过 10 次的概率是多少？

这个等待时间就是**几何分布**。它最重要的性质是**无记忆性**：过去的失败不改变未来的等待。它是连续指数分布的离散对应，在港大风格考题里极常见（尾部条件概率、“谁先满”、多个几何的最小值）。

## Step 1: Two conventions — lock the support first / 第一步：两种约定——先锁死支撑

There are **two** standard definitions. Exams and textbooks mix them; writing the wrong mean is an instant zero on that part.

存在**两套**标准定义。试卷与教材混用；均值写错会直接失分。

**Convention A — trial number of first success** (support $\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$)

$$\Pr(T=k) = (1-p)^{k-1}p, \qquad k=1,2,\ldots$$

Interpretation: $k-1$ failures, then one success.  
Mean: $E(T)=1/p$. Variance: $\mathrm{Var}(T)=(1-p)/p^{2}$.

**Convention B — number of failures before first success** (support $\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$)

$$\Pr(X=k) = (1-p)^{k}p, \qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots$$

Mean: $E(X)=(1-p)/p$. Same variance $(1-p)/p^{2}$.

Relation: if $T$ is Convention A and $X$ is Convention B with the same $p$, then $T = X+1$.

**In this node we use Convention A** (first-success trial number) unless a problem explicitly says “number of failures”. When you open a problem, **read the support** before writing any formula.

本节点默认用约定 A（首次成功的试验序号），除非题目明确写“失败次数”。打开题目时，**先看支撑**再写公式。

---

## Step 2: Derive the PMF from Bernoulli trials / 第二步：从 Bernoulli 试验推出 PMF

Assume independent trials, each success with probability $p\in(0,1)$.

For $T=k$ under Convention A:

- Trials $1,\ldots,k-1$ must fail: each contributes factor $q:=1-p$.
- Trial $k$ must succeed: factor $p$.

Independence multiplies the factors:

$$\Pr(T=k) = q^{k-1}p.$$

Check that it is a PMF:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} q^{k-1}p = p\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} q^{j} = p\cdot\frac{1}{1-q} = p\cdot\frac{1}{p} = 1.$$

(Geometric series — see [M0.5](M0.5.md).)

假设试验独立，每次成功概率 $p\in(0,1)$。约定 A 下 $T=k$：前 $k-1$ 次失败、第 $k$ 次成功，独立性给出 $\Pr(T=k)=q^{k-1}p$。级数求和验证是 PMF。

---

## Step 3: Mean via conditioning (or series) / 第三步：用条件期望（或级数）求均值

**Series way.**  
$$E(T)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k\,q^{k-1}p.$$

Use the identity $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k r^{k-1}=1/(1-r)^{2}$ for $|r|<1$:

$$E(T)=p\cdot\frac{1}{(1-q)^{2}}=p\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}}=\frac{1}{p}.$$

**Conditioning way (more useful for intuition).**  
After the first trial:

- With probability $p$, $T=1$.
- With probability $q$, you have used 1 trial and the remaining wait is an independent copy of $T$.

So

$$E(T)=p\cdot 1 + q\cdot\bigl(1+E(T)\bigr).$$

Solve: $E(T)=p+q+qE(T)\implies pE(T)=1\implies E(T)=1/p$.

Variance can be obtained similarly by conditioning on the first trial, or from $E(T(T-1))$ via the series for $\sum k(k-1)r^{k}$. Result: $\mathrm{Var}(T)=q/p^{2}$.

级数法用 $\sum k r^{k-1}=1/(1-r)^{2}$。条件法：第一次成功则 $T=1$，否则已用 1 次且剩余仍是独立的 $T$，解方程得 $E(T)=1/p$。方差 $\mathrm{Var}(T)=q/p^{2}$。

## Step 4: Survival function and CDF / 第四步：生存函数与 CDF

$$\Pr(T>k)=q^{k}, \qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots$$

(Why? The first $k$ trials are all failures.)

$$\Pr(T\le k)=1-q^{k}, \qquad F(k)=1-q^{k}\ \text{for integer }k\ge 0.$$

Exams often ask you to **derive** the CDF of Geo($p$) — write $\Pr(T\le k)=1-(1-p)^{k}$ and state the support.

$\Pr(T>k)=q^{k}$（前 $k$ 次全失败）。$F(k)=1-q^{k}$。试卷常要求**推导** Geo 的 CDF，写清支撑。

---

## Step 5: Memorylessness — statement and proof / 第五步：无记忆性——陈述与证明

**Statement.** For non-negative integers $m,n$,

$$\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=\Pr(T>n).$$

In words: given that the first success has not occurred in the first $m$ trials, the extra number of trials still needed has the **same** distribution as the original $T$. The process restarts.

**Proof.** By definition of conditional probability,

$$\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=\frac{\Pr(T>m+n)}{\Pr(T>m)}=\frac{q^{m+n}}{q^{m}}=q^{n}=\Pr(T>n).$$

That is the entire proof. The algebraic cancellation is the discrete counterpart of the exponential calculation $\Pr(X>s+t\mid X>s)=e^{-\lambda t}$.

**Equivalent form often used in exams:**

$$\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=\Pr(T>n),$$

or, shifting to the “remaining life”,

$$\Pr(T-m>n\mid T>m)=\Pr(T>n).$$

**陈述.** 对非负整数 $m,n$，$\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=\Pr(T>n)$。已等了 $m$ 次仍未成功，再需要的次数与原来同分布。

**证明.** $\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=q^{m+n}/q^{m}=q^{n}=\Pr(T>n)$。代数消去就是全部证明，对应指数分布的无记忆性计算。

## Step 6: Why memorylessness matters in exam problems / 第六步：无记忆性在考题里怎么用

**Example type 1 — pure conditioning on the tail.**  
(From past papers style.) $X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\Pr(X<8\mid X>4)=0.999$. Find $\Pr(5<X<7)$.

Use memorylessness (or direct survival):

$$\Pr(X\le 7\mid X>4)=\Pr(X\le 3)=1-q^{3},$$

so $\Pr(X<8\mid X>4)=1-q^{3}=0.999\implies q^{3}=0.001\implies q=0.1$, $p=0.9$.  
Then $\Pr(5<X<7)=\Pr(X=6)=q^{5}p=(0.1)^{5}(0.9)$.

(Always re-derive from survival if you distrust the shift.)

**Example type 2 — min of independent geometrics.**  
Independent $X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p_{i})$ (Convention A). Then

$$\Pr(\min_{i}X_{i}>k)=\prod_{i}\Pr(X_{i}>k)=\prod_{i}q_{i}^{k}=\Bigl(\prod_{i}q_{i}\Bigr)^{k},$$

so $\min_{i}X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Geo}\bigl(1-\prod q_{i}\bigr)$.  
This is the discrete analogue of “min of independent exponentials is exponential with summed rates”.

**Example type 3 — “A fills before B”.**  
Two independent streams of Bernoulli trials (e.g. passengers choosing bus A or B). Waiting until A has 3 more passengers is NegativeBinomial; the probability A reaches 3 before B reaches 3 reduces to a finite binomial calculation or to a negative-binomial CDF — but the building block is geometric waiting. See [M4.5](M4.5.md).

**类型 1**：尾部条件概率，用 $\Pr(T>m+n)/\Pr(T>m)=q^{n}$。  
**类型 2**：独立几何的最小值仍是几何，$p'=1-\prod q_{i}$。  
**类型 3**：竞争到达（谁先满）由几何/负二项拼出。

## Step 7: Link to the exponential / 第七步：与指数分布的关系

If you run Bernoulli trials every $\Delta t$ time units with success probability $p=\lambda\Delta t$ (small), the number of trials until first success, multiplied by $\Delta t$, converges to an $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$ waiting time. Memorylessness is preserved in the limit. That is why geometric and exponential share the same “no aging” intuition and why exam courses treat them as a pair ([M8.4](M8.4.md)).

若每隔 $\Delta t$ 做一次成功概率 $p=\lambda\Delta t$ 的 Bernoulli，首次成功时间乘 $\Delta t$ 在极限下成为 $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$。无记忆性在极限中保留，故几何与指数常成对出现。

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Geometric is “trials until first success” (or failures before it) — **support decides the mean**.  
2. Memorylessness is not a slogan: it is the equality $\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=q^{n}$.  
3. After any run of failures, the future is a fresh geometric; the past is irrelevant.  
4. Min of independent Geos is again Geo — useful for competing risks / first event.

1. 几何是“等到第一次成功”（或之前的失败次数）——**支撑决定均值**。  
2. 无记忆性是等式 $\Pr(T>m+n\mid T>m)=q^{n}$，不是口号。  
3. 任意失败串之后，未来仍是全新的几何。  
4. 独立几何的最小值仍是几何，用于竞争风险/第一次事件。

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Why it hurts |
|--------|----------------|
| Using $E=1/p$ when the problem counts **failures** | Off-by-one on every mean/variance question |
| Writing $\Pr(T=k)=q^{k}p$ on support $\{1,2,\ldots\}$ | Wrong PMF (that is Convention B) |
| Claiming memorylessness for binomial or hypergeometric | Only geometric (discrete) and exponential (continuous) among the usual named families |
| Forgetting independence when taking min of several waits | Product of survivals needs independence |
| Applying geometric formulas when $p$ changes over trials | Model breaks; need different tools |

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Let $T$ be the number of independent Bernoulli($p=1/3$) trials until the first success (Convention A).

(a) Find $\Pr(T>4)$.  
(b) Find $\Pr(T>7\mid T>4)$.  
(c) Find $E(T\mid T>4)$.

**Solution.**

(a) $\Pr(T>4)=q^{4}=(2/3)^{4}=16/81$.

(b) By memorylessness, $\Pr(T>7\mid T>4)=\Pr(T>3)=(2/3)^{3}=8/27$.  
(Directly: $\Pr(T>7)/\Pr(T>4)=q^{7}/q^{4}=q^{3}$.)

(c) Given $T>4$, the **remaining** wait $T-4$ is distributed as $T$. Thus

$$E(T\mid T>4)=4+E(T)=4+1/(1/3)=4+3=7.$$

(The “4” is already spent; the future expectation is still $1/p$.)

**问题.** $T$ 为 Bernoulli($p=1/3$) 直到首次成功的次数（约定 A）。

(a) $\Pr(T>4)=(2/3)^{4}=16/81$。  
(b) 无记忆性 $\Pr(T>7\mid T>4)=\Pr(T>3)=8/27$。  
(c) 给定 $T>4$，剩余 $T-4$ 与 $T$ 同分布，故 $E(T\mid T>4)=4+3=7$。

## Practice / 练习

1. $T\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$. Show that $\Pr(T=m+k\mid T>m)=\Pr(T=k)$ for $k\ge 1$. (Stronger form of memorylessness on the point masses.)

2. Independent $T_{1}\sim\mathrm{Geo}(0.3)$, $T_{2}\sim\mathrm{Geo}(0.5)$ (Convention A). Find the distribution of $\min(T_{1},T_{2})$ and $E[\min(T_{1},T_{2})]$.

3. (Exam flavour) For $X\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)$ on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$, it is given that $\Pr(X<8\mid X>4)=0.999$. Find $\Pr(5<X<7)$.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $\Pr(T=m+k\mid T>m)=\Pr(T=m+k)/\Pr(T>m)=q^{m+k-1}p/q^{m}=q^{k-1}p=\Pr(T=k)$.

2. $\Pr(\min>k)=(0.7)^{k}(0.5)^{k}=(0.35)^{k}$, so $\min\sim\mathrm{Geo}(1-0.35)=\mathrm{Geo}(0.65)$, mean $1/0.65=20/13$.

3. $1-q^{3}=0.999\Rightarrow q=0.1$, $p=0.9$. $\Pr(X=6)=q^{5}p=0.9\times 10^{-5}$.

</details>
