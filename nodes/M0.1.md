# M0.1 Sets and Event Language / 集合与事件语言

**Prerequisites / 前置**: none

**Source anchors**: set algebra for events; De Morgan; translating verbal events into set operations.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Probability is built on sets. Before any numbers, we need a precise language: element, subset, union, intersection, complement, difference, and Venn pictures. Without it we cannot even state what an “event” is.

概率建立在集合上。先要有语言：元素、子集、并、交、补、差与 Venn 图。没有这些就无法说清「事件」是什么。

---

## Step 1: Basic operations / 第一步：基本运算

Let $\Omega$ be the universe (later the sample space). For $A,B\subseteq\Omega$:

- Union: $A\cup B=\{x:x\in A\text{ or }x\in B\}$
- Intersection: $A\cap B=\{x:x\in A\text{ and }x\in B\}$
- Complement: $A^{c}=\Omega\setminus A$
- Difference: $A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}$

**Disjoint (互斥):** $A\cap B=\emptyset$.  
**Exhaustive (穷尽):** $A\cup B=\Omega$.

---

## Step 2: De Morgan laws / 第二步：德摩根律

$$
(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c},\qquad (A\cap B)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}.
$$

These swap union/intersection under complement and are used constantly for “neither” and “not both”.

并补互换、交补互换；「既不…也不…」「并非两者都…」时直接用。

---

## Step 3: Events as sets / 第三步：事件就是集合

In probability, outcomes live in a sample space $\Omega$; an **event** is a subset of $\Omega$ to which we assign probability. Verbal phrases must be translated into set operations:

| English / 中文 | Set expression |
|----------------|----------------|
| At least one of $A,B$ | $A\cup B$ |
| Both $A$ and $B$ | $A\cap B$ |
| Neither $A$ nor $B$ | $A^{c}\cap B^{c}=(A\cup B)^{c}$ |
| Exactly one of $A,B$ | $(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)$ |
| $A$ but not $B$ | $A\setminus B$ |

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

$\Omega=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, $A=\{1,2,3\}$, $B=\{3,4,5\}$.

- $A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$
- $A\cap B=\{3\}$
- $A^{c}=\{4,5,6\}$
- $(A\cup B)^{c}=\{6\}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}$

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Events are sets; probability is a set function.  
2. Verbal “at least / exactly / neither” must be written as $\cup,\cap,{}^{c}$ before computing.  
3. De Morgan is the bridge between “or” and “and” under negation.  
4. Disjoint = empty intersection; exhaustive = union is the whole space.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Treating “at least one” as intersection | Use union (or complement of “none”) |
| Forgetting De Morgan when negating “or” | $(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}$ |
| Writing $A\setminus B$ as $A-B$ without defining difference | Prefer $A\cap B^{c}$ |
| Assuming two events always partition $\Omega$ | Check both disjoint **and** exhaustive |

---

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** If $A\subset B$, which is always true?

A. $A\cup B=A$  
B. $A\cup B=B$  
C. $A\cap B=B$  
D. $A^{c}\subset B^{c}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Because every element of $A$ is already in $B$, the union adds nothing new: $A\cup B=B$.  
Intersection is $A$, not $B$. Complement reverses inclusion, so $B^{c}\subset A^{c}$, not the other way.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Which expression equals “neither $A$ nor $B$”?

A. $A^{c}\cup B^{c}$  
B. $A^{c}\cap B^{c}$  
C. $(A\cap B)^{c}$  
D. $A\setminus B$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

“Neither” means not in $A$ **and** not in $B$, i.e. $A^{c}\cap B^{c}$.  
By De Morgan this is also $(A\cup B)^{c}$.  
$A^{c}\cup B^{c}$ is “not both”; $(A\cap B)^{c}$ is “not (both)”.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** $\Omega=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $A=\{1,2\}$, $B=\{2,3\}$. What is $A\setminus B$?

A. $\{1\}$  
B. $\{2\}$  
C. $\{1,2\}$  
D. $\{1,3\}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}=\{1,2\}\cap\{1,4\}=\{1\}$.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**4.** Which pair is always true by De Morgan?

A. $(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}$  
B. $(A\cap B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}$  
C. $(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}$  
D. $A\cup A^{c}=\emptyset$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard De Morgan: complement of union is intersection of complements.  
The other listed identities are false.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**5.** “Exactly one of $A$ or $B$ occurs” is best written as

A. $A\cup B$  
B. $A\cap B$  
C. $(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)$  
D. $A^{c}\cap B^{c}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Exactly one = ($A$ but not $B$) or ($B$ but not $A$) = $(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)$.  
Union allows both; intersection is both; neither is the last option.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**6.** If $A$ and $B$ are disjoint and exhaustive, then

A. $A\cup B=\emptyset$ and $A\cap B=\Omega$  
B. $A\cup B=\Omega$ and $A\cap B=\emptyset$  
C. $A=B$  
D. $A^{c}=B^{c}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Disjoint $\Rightarrow A\cap B=\emptyset$; exhaustive $\Rightarrow A\cup B=\Omega$.  
They form a partition of $\Omega$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** Translate: “$A$ occurs but $B$ does not.”

A. $A\cup B^{c}$  
B. $A\cap B^{c}$  
C. $A^{c}\cap B$  
D. $A\setminus B^{c}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

In $A$ and not in $B$: $A\cap B^{c}$ (same as $A\setminus B$).  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
