# M6.3 Independent Trials vs Markov / 独立试验与马氏链

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.6](M1.6.md), [M6.1](M6.1.md)

**Source anchors**: i.i.d. as Markov with identical rows; geometric waiting; dependence through state.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Independent trials are a special Markov chain: the next outcome does not depend on the current state value. Many waiting-time problems still use geometric laws; Markov dependence changes those calculations.

独立试验是特殊马氏链（行相同）。等待时间在独立时用几何；有状态依赖则不同。

---

## Step 1: i.i.d. as Markov / 第一步：i.i.d. 作为马氏

If every row of $P$ equals the same distribution $q$, then $X_{t+1}$ is independent of $X_t$ and $\pi^{(t)}=q$ for all $t$ after the first draw from $q$.

---

## Step 2: Geometric waiting / 第二步：几何等待

Trials until first success, success probability $p$ each time independent: $\Pr(T=k)=(1-p)^{k-1}p$. Markov with two states {continue, done} recovers the same law when the “continue” row restarts the clock.

---

## Step 3: When Markov is not i.i.d. / 第三步：非独立马氏

$\alpha\neq 1-\beta$ in general ⇒ consecutive states correlated. Run lengths and return times need the full chain structure.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. i.i.d. ⊂ Markov (identical rows).  
2. Geometric waiting ↔ independent restarts.  
3. Correlation of successive states is the generic Markov case.  
4. Do not use geometric formulas blindly when transitions depend on state.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using geometric success on a dependent chain | Check independence of trials |
| Thinking every Markov chain has independent increments | Only special $P$ |
| Forgetting stationary may still exist under dependence | Solve $\pi P=\pi$ |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** i.i.d. trials correspond to a Markov chain with

A. different rows  
B. identical rows  
C. $\alpha=0$ only  
D. no stationary law

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Next independent of current.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Geometric waiting assumes

A. Markov dependence  
B. independent trials with constant success probability  
C. $\pi P=\pi$ only  
D. two absorbing states

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

i.i.d. Bernoulli.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** $\Pr(T=k)=(1-p)^{k-1}p$ is the law of

A. binomial count  
B. trials until first success  
C. stationary $\pi$  
D. $P_{00}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Geometric (support $1,2,\ldots$).  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** If consecutive states are correlated, successive trials are

A. always independent  
B. not independent  
C. geometric always  
D. impossible

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Generic Markov.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** A two-state chain with identical rows has

A. $\alpha=1-\beta$ in the standard parametrisation sense that rows match  
B. necessarily absorbing  
C. no $P$  
D. $\pi$ undefined

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Rows equal ⇒ special $\alpha,\beta$ relation.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**6.** Using geometric formula on a weather chain with sticky states is

A. always correct  
B. typically wrong (dependence)  
C. required by Markov  
D. the definition of $P$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Independence fails.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** After one draw from stationary $\pi$ on an i.i.d. chain, the law of $X_t$ is

A. changing forever  
B. still $\pi$ for all $t$  
C. concentrated at 0  
D. $P$ itself

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Already invariant.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** Markov is strictly larger than i.i.d. because

A. rows may differ  
B. $P$ must be $I$  
C. only continuous time  
D. $\pi$ must be uniform

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

State-dependent transitions.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>
