# M2.2 With Replacement, Ordered / 有放回有序抽样

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M2.1](M2.1.md)

**Source anchors**: $n^{k}$; independent trials; passwords; die sequences.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Sequences of length $k$ where each position is chosen from $n$ options and repeats are allowed: passwords, die rolls, sampling with replacement when order is recorded.

长度为 $k$、每位置 $n$ 选一、允许重复的序列：密码、掷骰、有放回且记录顺序。

---

## Step 1: Count / 第一步：计数

$$n^{k}.$$

Each of $k$ positions has $n$ independent choices (in the combinatorial sense of the product rule).

---

## Step 2: Probability under uniformity / 第二步：等可能概率

If every sequence is equally likely, $\Pr(\text{specific sequence})=n^{-k}$.  
Under independent uniform trials this matches the product of per-trial probabilities.

---

## Step 3: Hand checks / 第三步：手算

Die 3 times: $6^{3}=216$ outcomes.  
$\Pr(\text{all sixes})=1/216$.  
$\Pr(\text{first face even})=1/2$ still, by counting $3\cdot 6^{2}/6^{3}$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Product rule with replacement and order → $n^{k}$.  
2. Uniform on sequences ⇒ each sequence has probability $n^{-k}$.  
3. This is the combinatorial skeleton of i.i.d. discrete trials.  
4. Contrast with $P(n,k)$ when repeats are forbidden.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using $\binom{n}{k}$ for ordered with-replacement | Wrong cell of the table |
| Using $P(n,k)$ when repeats are allowed | $P$ forbids repeats |
| Forgetting order when sequences matter | Sequences are ordered |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** Die rolled 3 times. Number of outcomes:

A. $18$  
B. $120$  
C. $216$  
D. $\binom{6}{3}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$6^{3}=216$.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**2.** $n^{k}$ counts

A. unordered without replacement  
B. ordered with replacement  
C. only $k=n$  
D. permutations of $n$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** Password length 2 from $\{A,B,C\}$, repetition allowed. Number:

A. $6$  
B. $9$  
C. $3$  
D. $\binom{3}{2}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$3^{2}=9$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** Under uniform sequences of length $k$ from $n$, $\Pr(\text{one fixed sequence})=$

A. $1/n$  
B. $1/k$  
C. $n^{-k}$  
D. $\binom{n}{k}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

One of $n^{k}$ equally likely.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**5.** $P(n,k)$ vs $n^{k}$: $P(n,k)$ is appropriate when

A. repeats are allowed  
B. repeats are forbidden and order matters  
C. order does not matter  
D. $k>n$ always

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Permutations without replacement.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** Two fair coin flips, ordered. $|\Omega|=$

A. $2$  
B. $3$  
C. $4$  
D. $\binom{2}{1}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$2^{2}=4$.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**7.** Sampling with replacement, ordered, is the combinatorial model for

A. poker hands  
B. i.i.d. discrete trials  
C. combinations only  
D. stars and bars only

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Product structure matches independence.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** $10^{4}$ equals the number of

A. 4-digit PINs from 0–9 with leading zeros allowed  
B. 4-subsets of 10  
C. permutations of 10 take 4  
D. $\binom{10}{4}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Ordered with replacement (digits).  
Answer: **A**.
</details>
