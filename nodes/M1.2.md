# M1.2 Complement, Union, Inclusion-Exclusion / 补事件与包含排除

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.1 Sample Space, Events, Probability Axioms](M1.1.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3 inclusion-exclusion; “at least one” via complement; derangement / matching as IE applications.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Many exam events are phrased as “at least one …” or “A or B or C”. Listing outcomes explodes; **complement** and **inclusion-exclusion** turn them into additions and subtractions of probabilities you already know.

很多题是「至少一个…」或「A 或 B 或 C」。枚举会爆炸；**补事件**与**包含排除**把它们变成已知概率的加减。

---

## Step 1: Complement / 第一步：补事件

$$\Pr(A^{c})=1-\Pr(A).$$

**Standard move for “at least one”:**

$$\Pr(\text{at least one success})=1-\Pr(\text{none}).$$

Often $\Pr(\text{none})$ factors under independence (product of failure probabilities). This is usually shorter than summing the binomial tail.

「至少一个」= $1-$「全无」。独立时「全无」常可写成失败概率之积。

---

## Step 2: Two-event inclusion-exclusion / 第二步：两事件

$$\Pr(A\cup B)=\Pr(A)+\Pr(B)-\Pr(A\cap B).$$

Why subtract? $A$ and $B$ both count the outcomes in $A\cap B$; subtracting once restores the correct total.

$A$ 与 $B$ 都把 $A\cap B$ 里的结果算进去了，减一次避免双计。

---

## Step 3: Three events / 第三步：三事件

$$\Pr(A\cup B\cup C)=\sum\Pr(A_i)-\sum\Pr(A_i\cap A_j)+\Pr(A\cap B\cap C).$$

Pattern: add singles, subtract pairwise intersections, add back the triple. For $n$ events the general inclusion-exclusion alternates over $k$-fold intersections with sign $(-1)^{k+1}$.

加单项、减两两交、加回三重交；一般 $n$ 事件按交的阶数正负交替。

---

## Step 4: De Morgan / 第四步：德摩根

$$(A\cup B)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}, \qquad (A\cap B)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}.$$

So $\Pr(A^{c}\cup B^{c})=1-\Pr(A\cap B)$, useful when a problem asks for “neither” or “not both”.

---

## Step 5: Hand numbers / 第五步：数字

$\Pr(A)=0.3$, $\Pr(B)=0.4$, $\Pr(A\cap B)=0.1$.  
$\Pr(A\cup B)=0.6$, $\Pr(\text{neither})=1-0.6=0.4$.

$\Pr(A)=0.5$, $\Pr(B)=0.4$, $\Pr(A\cup B)=0.7$ ⇒ $\Pr(A\cap B)=0.2$; $\Pr(A^{c}\cup B^{c})=1-0.2=0.8$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. “At least one” → complement of “none”.  
2. Inclusion-exclusion corrects double-counting of intersections.  
3. De Morgan swaps unions/intersections under complement.  
4. Independent “none” often multiplies; dependent cases need joint information.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Forgetting to subtract $\Pr(A\cap B)$ | Always IE for unions |
| Complement of the wrong event | Write “none” carefully |
| Applying IE signs incorrectly for 3+ events | $+\;-\;+$ pattern |
| Using $|A|/|\Omega|$ when defining $A$ was the hard part | Focus on set description first |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** $\Pr(A)=0.5$, $\Pr(B)=0.4$, $\Pr(A\cup B)=0.7$. Find $\Pr(A\cap B)$ and $\Pr(A^{c}\cup B^{c})$.

**Solution.** $\Pr(A\cap B)=0.5+0.4-0.7=0.2$. $\Pr(A^{c}\cup B^{c})=1-0.2=0.8$.

## Practice / 练习

1. Independent events $A,B$ with $\Pr(A)=0.3$, $\Pr(B)=0.5$. $\Pr(A\cup B)$?

2. Why is $\Pr(\text{at least one head in 3 fair coins})=1-(1/2)^{3}$?

3. Three events: write the IE formula and check the sign of the triple term.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $0.3+0.5-0.3\cdot 0.5=0.65$.

2. Complement of “no heads” = all tails, probability $(1/2)^{3}$ under independence.

3. Triple term has sign $+$.

</details>
