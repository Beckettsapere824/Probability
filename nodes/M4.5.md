# M4.5 Negative Binomial — Waiting for the r-th Success / 负二项：等到第 r 次成功

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M4.2 Binomial](M4.2.md), [M4.4 Geometric](M4.4.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A waiting times; exam “A fills before B”, packs until 3 gift cards of type A, NB–Binomial relation.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Geometric waits for the **first** success. Life is rarely that kind: you need the **third** defective item, the **r-th** head, or enough passengers of type A to fill a minibus. On which trial does the r-th success land?

That random trial number is the **negative binomial**. It is the sum of r independent geometrics, so every geometric intuition (support conventions, memoryless building blocks, competing waits) scales up — and appears in exam problems about “how many packs until 3 cards of type A” and “A reaches 3 before B reaches 3”.

几何等的是**第一次**成功。现实里你往往要第 **r** 次：第 3 个次品、第 r 次正面、或 A 类乘客凑满一辆车。第 r 次成功落在第几次试验上？

这个试验编号就是**负二项**。它是 r 个独立几何之和，几何的支撑约定、无记忆积木、竞争等待全部可放大——并出现在「买多少包才凑齐 3 张 A 类卡」「A 先满 3 还是 B 先满 3」这类考题里。

---

## Step 1: Two conventions again / 第一步：又是两套约定

**Convention A — trial number of the r-th success** (this node’s default)

Support: $n=r,r+1,r+2,\ldots$

$$\Pr(T=n)=\binom{n-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{n-r}.$$

Story: in the first $n-1$ trials there are **exactly** $r-1$ successes (binomial), and trial $n$ is a success.

Mean: $E(T)=r/p$. Variance: $\mathrm{Var}(T)=r(1-p)/p^{2}$.

**Convention B — number of failures before the r-th success**

Support: $k=0,1,2,\ldots$

$$\Pr(X=k)=\binom{k+r-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{k}.$$

Mean: $E(X)=r(1-p)/p$. Same variance $r(1-p)/p^{2}$.

Relation: $T=X+r$.

**Exam rule:** read whether the random variable is “number of trials” or “number of failures / packs beyond r” before choosing the mean formula.

**考试规则：** 先分清变量是「试验次数」还是「失败次数」，再选均值公式。

---

## Step 2: Derive the PMF (Convention A) / 第二步：推导 PMF

For $T=n$:

1. Among trials $1,\ldots,n-1$: exactly $r-1$ successes and $(n-1)-(r-1)=n-r$ failures.  
   Number of such binary sequences: $\binom{n-1}{r-1}$.  
   Probability of any one of them: $p^{r-1}(1-p)^{n-r}$.

2. Trial $n$ is a success: factor $p$.

Multiply:

$$\Pr(T=n)=\binom{n-1}{r-1}p^{r-1}(1-p)^{n-r}\cdot p=\binom{n-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{n-r}.$$

Special case $r=1$: $\binom{n-1}{0}p(1-p)^{n-1}=(1-p)^{n-1}p$, the geometric of [M4.4](M4.4.md).

---

## Step 3: Sum of i.i.d. geometrics / 第三步：独立几何之和

Let $T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}$ be i.i.d. Geo($p$) under Convention A (trials until first success). The waiting time until the r-th success is

$$T=T_{1}+\cdots+T_{r}.$$

Why? After each success the process restarts (memoryless), so the gap to the next success is a fresh geometric. Linearity gives

$$E(T)=r\cdot\frac{1}{p}=\frac{r}{p}, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(T)=r\cdot\frac{1-p}{p^{2}}.$$

This is the cleanest way to remember mean and variance — no series required once you trust geometric.

每次成功后过程重启（无记忆），到下一次成功的间隔是新的几何。线性性直接给出均值方差。

---

## Step 4: Relation to binomial (exam lever) / 第四步：与二项的关系（考试杠杆）

$$\Pr(T>n)=\Pr(\text{fewer than }r\text{ successes in }n\text{ trials})=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}.$$

Equivalently,

$$\Pr(T\le n)=\Pr(\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p)\ge r).$$

**Use case in papers:** “probability you need more than 10 packs to get 3 type-A cards” becomes “fewer than 3 successes in 10 Bernoulli($1/4$) trials” — a short binomial sum, not an infinite negative-binomial tail.

试卷用法：「买超过 10 包才凑齐 3 张 A」=「10 次 Bernoulli 中成功少于 3 次」，化成有限二项和。

---

## Step 5: Competing waits (“A fills before B”) / 第五步：竞争等待

Passengers arrive one by one; each chooses A with probability $\alpha$, B with $1-\alpha$, independently. A needs 3 more seats, B needs 3 more. Probability A fills first =

$$\Pr(\text{at least 3 of the next 5 arrivals choose A})$$

because the race ends when either side reaches 3, so within 5 arrivals someone must have won, and A wins iff A got to 3 before B got to 3, which is equivalent to A having ≥3 in the first 5 of the race (standard negative-binomial / binomial duality).

Alternatively compute via NB probabilities directly. Past papers ask numerical values to 4 decimals — carry the binomial coefficients carefully.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. NB($r,p$) = sum of r i.i.d. Geo($p$) gaps.  
2. Support convention decides whether mean is $r/p$ or $r(1-p)/p$.  
3. Tail of NB ↔ CDF of Binomial — convert when a finite sum is easier.  
4. “How many trials until r successes” is the same family as “how many failures before r successes”, shifted by r.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| $\binom{n}{r}$ instead of $\binom{n-1}{r-1}$ | Last trial fixed as success; choose $r-1$ successes in the previous $n-1$ |
| Mean $r/p$ on a failure-count variable | Off by the additive $r$ |
| Summing the NB series for a “more than n trials” probability | Switch to binomial CDF |
| Treating dependent trials as NB | Independence of Bernoulli trials is required |

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Each pack contains a card of type A with probability $1/4$, independently. Let $T$ be the number of packs needed to obtain 3 type-A cards (Convention A, $r=3$).

(a) Write $\Pr(T=5)$.  
(b) Find $\Pr(T>10)$ using a binomial probability.  
(c) $E(T)$?

**Solution.**

(a) $\Pr(T=5)=\binom{4}{2}(1/4)^{3}(3/4)^{2}=6\cdot(1/64)\cdot(9/16)=54/1024=27/512$.

(b) $\Pr(T>10)=\Pr(\mathrm{Binomial}(10,1/4)\le 2)$  
$=\binom{10}{0}(3/4)^{10}+\binom{10}{1}(1/4)(3/4)^{9}+\binom{10}{2}(1/4)^{2}(3/4)^{8}$.  
(Numerically ≈ 0.5256 in past-paper answers.)

(c) $E(T)=3/(1/4)=12$.

2. Show that if $T\sim\mathrm{NB}(r,p)$ (Convention A) and $r=1$, then $T$ is Geo($p$).

3. Independent Geo waits $T_{1},T_{2}\sim\mathrm{Geo}(p)$ (Convention A). What is the law of $T_{1}+T_{2}$?

<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $\Pr(T=4)=\binom{3}{1}(1/2)^{2}(1/2)^{2}=3/16$.

3. $\mathrm{NB}(2,p)$ by the sum-of-geometrics representation.
