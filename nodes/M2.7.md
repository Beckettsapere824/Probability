# M2.7 Complementary Counting / 补集计数

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M2.1](M2.1.md), [M1.2](M1.2.md)

**Source anchors**: total minus “none”; at least one; surjections sometimes via IE.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

“At least one …” is often easier as **total − none** than by summing the positive cases.

「至少一个…」常用 **总数 − 全无**，比直接加正案例更短。

---

## Step 1: Pattern / 第一步：模式

$$\#\{\text{at least one property}\}=\#\{\text{all}\}-\#\{\text{none of the property}\}.$$

In probability under uniformity: $\Pr(\text{at least one})=1-\Pr(\text{none})$.

---

## Step 2: Hand checks / 第二步：手算

5-bit strings with at least one 1: $2^{5}-1=31$ (subtract the all-zero string).  
Functions from a set of size $k$ to a set of size $n$ that miss at least one value: harder — often inclusion-exclusion rather than a single complement.

---

## Step 3: Link to probability / 第三步：与概率的联系

Same logic as [M1.2](M1.2.md) complement. Counting version supplies the numerator/denominator for classical probability.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Prefer complement when “none” is simple.  
2. Uniform probability: divide the same counts.  
3. “At least two” may need more than one complement step or IE.  
4. Do not subtract the wrong set.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Subtracting “at least one” instead of “none” | Define the easy set carefully |
| Forgetting the empty/all-zero case | Enumerate extremes |
| Using complement when “none” is hard | Switch to direct count or IE |

---

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** 5-bit strings with at least one 1:

A. $16$  
B. $31$  
C. $32$  
D. $30$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$2^{5}-1=31$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Complementary counting uses

A. total − none  
B. only inclusion-exclusion  
C. $n^{k}$ only  
D. Bayes

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition of the method.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**3.** $\Pr(\text{at least one head in 3 fair coins})=$

A. $1/8$  
B. $7/8$  
C. $1/2$  
D. $3/8$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$1-(1/2)^{3}$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** Number of outcomes in $\{1,\ldots,6\}^{2}$ with at least one 6:

A. $11$  
B. $12$  
C. $36-25=11$  
D. $36$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$36-25=11$.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**5.** When “none” is complicated, prefer

A. still force complement  
B. direct count or inclusion-exclusion  
C. ignore the problem  
D. only $P(n,k)$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Complement is a tool, not mandatory.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** 4-letter strings from $\{A,B,C\}$ with at least one $A$:

A. $3^{4}$  
B. $3^{4}-2^{4}=65$  
C. $2^{4}$  
D. $4$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Total minus no $A$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** Complement of “at least one success” is

A. at least two successes  
B. zero successes  
C. all successes  
D. independence

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

None.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** Classical $\Pr(\text{at least one})=$ under uniformity

A. $(\#\text{at least one})/(\#\text{all})$  
B. only $1$  
C. $P(n,k)$  
D. stars and bars

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Same ratio as any classical event.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>
