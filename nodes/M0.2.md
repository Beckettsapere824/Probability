# M0.2 Product and Sum Rules / 乘法原理与加法原理

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.1 Sets and Event Language](M0.1.md)

**Source anchors**: fundamental counting principles; sequential vs mutually exclusive choices.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

How many ways can a sequence of independent choices be completed? How do we total the number of mutually exclusive alternatives? These two principles are the foundation of almost all counting arguments that later become classical probability.

多步独立选择有多少种完成方式？互斥方案如何合计？这两条原理是古典概型计数的基础。

---

## Step 1: Product rule (multiplication principle) / 第一步：乘法原理

If a procedure consists of $k$ ordered stages, and stage $i$ can be done in $n_i$ ways **regardless of previous choices**, then the total number of outcomes is

$$
n_1\cdot n_2\cdots n_k.
$$

Typical pattern: “first choose …, then choose …”.

---

## Step 2: Sum rule (addition principle) / 第二步：加法原理

If a collection of possibilities can be partitioned into $m$ **mutually exclusive** (disjoint) classes of sizes $n_1,\ldots,n_m$, then the total is

$$
n_1+n_2+\cdots+n_m.
$$

Typical pattern: “either type A or type B (no overlap)”.

---

## Step 3: When they mix / 第三步：混合使用

Often one first partitions by cases (sum), then counts each case by ordered stages (product). Always check: Are the stages independent of earlier choices? Are the cases truly disjoint?

先用加法分情形，再用乘法算每情形内部；检查独立性与互斥性。

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

- 3 shirts, 2 pants (any shirt with any pants): $3\times 2=6$ outfits.  
- Password: 2 letters (26 options each) followed by 1 digit (10 options): $26\times 26\times 10=6760$.  
- Either a red or a blue ball is drawn from two separate boxes that share no balls: add the two box counts.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Product = sequential independent choices; Sum = mutually exclusive alternatives.  
2. “Regardless of previous” is the key assumption of the product rule.  
3. Overcounting usually means the cases were not disjoint or order was treated inconsistently.  
4. Almost every classical counting problem is a mixture of these two rules.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Multiplying when choices are mutually exclusive | Use sum |
| Adding when stages are sequential | Use product |
| Ignoring that later stage depends on earlier choice | Condition or re-partition |
| Double-counting the same object in two “cases” | Enforce disjoint partition |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** A meal consists of 1 soup (4 choices) and 1 main (5 choices). How many different meals?

A. $4+5=9$  
B. $4\times 5=20$  
C. $4\times 5-1=19$  
D. $5^{4}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Two sequential independent stages: any soup with any main. Product rule gives $4\times 5=20$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** A student may take either Mathematics (3 elective slots) or Physics (2 elective slots); the two programmes share no courses. Total elective packages?

A. $3\times 2=6$  
B. $3+2=5$  
C. $3^{2}$  
D. $5!$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

The two programmes are mutually exclusive alternatives → sum rule: $3+2=5$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** A password is 1 letter (26) followed by 2 digits (10 each). Number of passwords?

A. $26+10+10$  
B. $26\times 10\times 10$  
C. $26\times 10^{2}/2$  
D. $36^{3}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Three ordered stages, each independent of previous: $26\times 10\times 10=2600$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** From a set of 5 distinct books you first choose a position on a shelf (3 positions) then place one book. Ways?

A. $5+3$  
B. $5\times 3$  
C. $P(5,3)$  
D. $\binom{5}{3}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Position then book: product $3\times 5=15$. (If order of selection of books also mattered further it would become a permutation, but the question only asks position + one book.)  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** Which statement correctly describes the product rule?

A. Add the number of ways of mutually exclusive options  
B. Multiply the number of ways of sequential independent stages  
C. Divide by the number of orders  
D. Always use factorials

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition of the product (multiplication) principle.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** Two disjoint boxes contain 4 and 7 balls respectively. You draw one ball from exactly one box. Total outcomes?

A. $4\times 7=28$  
B. $4+7=11$  
C. $11!$  
D. $\binom{11}{1}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Choose which box (mutually exclusive) then a ball from that box: sum of the two sizes = 11.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** A 3-letter code uses letters A–Z, repetition allowed, order matters. How many codes?

A. $26+26+26$  
B. $26\times 25\times 24$  
C. $26^{3}$  
D. $\binom{26}{3}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Three sequential stages, each 26 options (repetition allowed) → $26^{3}$.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>
