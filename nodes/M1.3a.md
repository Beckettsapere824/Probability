# M1.3a Conditional Complement and Conditional Independence / 条件补事件与条件独立

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.3](M1.3.md), [M1.6](M1.6.md)

**Source anchors**: working inside $\{C\}$; $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A\mid C)$; conditional independence.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Once we condition on $C$, the world is the restricted space $C$. Complements, unions, and independence must be restated relative to $C$ — not relative to $\Omega$.

以 $C$ 为条件后，世界变成 $C$。补、并、独立都要相对于 $C$ 重新表述，而不是相对于 $\Omega$。

---

## Step 1: Conditional complement / 第一步：条件补事件

$$
\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A\mid C)\qquad(\Pr(C)>0).
$$

Proof: $A$ and $A^{c}$ partition $\Omega$, so their intersections with $C$ partition $C$; apply the definition of conditional probability.

Same pattern for “neither” inside $C$: $\Pr(A^{c}\cap B^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A\cup B\mid C)$ after De Morgan inside $C$.

---

## Step 2: Conditional independence / 第二步：条件独立

$A$ and $B$ are **conditionally independent given $C$** when

$$
\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)=\Pr(A\mid C)\Pr(B\mid C).
$$

This is independence in the conditional world. It does **not** imply ordinary independence, and ordinary independence does not imply conditional independence.

---

## Step 3: Formulas still work inside $C$ / 第三步：条件世界里公式仍可用

Inclusion-exclusion, complement, and monotonicity all hold for the conditional probability measure $\Pr(\,\cdot\mid C)$.

Example: $\Pr(A\cup B\mid C)=\Pr(A\mid C)+\Pr(B\mid C)-\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)$.

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

$\Pr(A\mid C)=0.4$ ⇒ $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=0.6$.

If $\Pr(A\mid C)=0.5$, $\Pr(B\mid C)=0.5$, and conditional independence, then $\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)=0.25$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Conditioning replaces $\Omega$ by $C$; every identity is re-applied there.  
2. Conditional independence is independence of the conditional measure.  
3. Ordinary independence and conditional independence are different statements.  
4. $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A\mid C)$ is the most used one-line rewrite.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Writing $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A)$ | Must condition the second term too |
| Assuming independence ⇒ conditional independence | Counterexamples exist (and vice versa) |
| Using $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$ inside a conditional problem | Use conditional versions |
| Forgetting $\Pr(C)>0$ | Definition requires positive conditioning event |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** $\Pr(A\mid C)=0.4$. Then $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=$

A. $0.4$  
B. $0.6$  
C. $1$  
D. cannot tell

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$1-0.4=0.6$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Conditional independence of $A,B$ given $C$ means

A. $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$  
B. $\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)=\Pr(A\mid C)\Pr(B\mid C)$  
C. $\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A\mid C)$  
D. $\Pr(C)=1$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition in the conditional world.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** Which is always true when $\Pr(C)>0$?

A. $\Pr(A\cup B\mid C)=\Pr(A\mid C)+\Pr(B\mid C)$  
B. $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=1-\Pr(A\mid C)$  
C. $\Pr(A\mid C)=\Pr(A)$  
D. $\Pr(A\cap C)=\Pr(A)$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Conditional complement identity.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** If $A$ and $B$ are conditionally independent given $C$, and $\Pr(A\mid C)=\Pr(B\mid C)=0.5$, then $\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)=$

A. $0$  
B. $0.25$  
C. $0.5$  
D. $1$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Product of the conditional probabilities.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** Ordinary independence of $A$ and $B$

A. always implies conditional independence given any $C$  
B. never relates to conditional independence  
C. is a different statement; neither implies the other in general  
D. requires $\Pr(C)=0$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard fact: the two notions are distinct.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**6.** $\Pr(A\cup B\mid C)$ equals

A. $\Pr(A\mid C)+\Pr(B\mid C)-\Pr(A\cap B\mid C)$

C. $\Pr(A\mid C)\Pr(B\mid C)$  
D. $1$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Inclusion-exclusion under the conditional measure.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**7.** If $\Pr(A^{c}\mid C)=0.3$, then $\Pr(A\mid C)=$

B. $0.7$

D. cannot tell

<details>

$1-0.3=0.7$.

</details>

**8.** De Morgan inside $C$: $\Pr(A^{c}\cap B^{c}\mid C)=$

A. $1-\Pr(A\cup B\mid C)$  
B. $1-\Pr(A\cap B)$  
C. $\Pr(A^{c})\Pr(B^{c})$  
D. $\Pr(C)$

<details>
