# M1.3 Conditional Probability and Multiplication Rule / 条件概率与乘法公式

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.1 Sample Space, Events, Axioms](M1.1.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3 §3.2; two-coin “at least one head”; die restriction; chain rule for three events.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

You learn something about the experiment and must **update** probabilities. “Given that the die showed an even face, what is the chance it is a six?” The relevant sample space shrinks to the conditioning event; probabilities are re-weighted on that smaller world.

你先知道试验的一部分信息，再**更新**概率。「已知骰子是偶数点，是六点的机会多大？」有效样本空间缩到条件事件上，概率在该小世界上重新加权。

---

## Step 1: Definition / 第一步：定义

If $\Pr(B)>0$,

$$\Pr(A\mid B)=\frac{\Pr(A\cap B)}{\Pr(B)}.$$

- Numerator: probability both happen.  
- Denominator: probability of the **conditioning** event (not $\Omega$).

On the restricted world “$B$ occurred”, the set function $\Pr(\cdot\mid B)$ still satisfies the probability axioms (non-negativity, normalisation on $B$, countable additivity for disjoint subsets of $B$).

分母是条件事件，不是全集。在「$B$ 已发生」的世界上，$\Pr(\cdot\mid B)$ 仍满足概率公理。

---

## Step 2: Multiplication (chain) rule / 第二步：乘法（链式）公式

Rearrange the definition:

$$\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A\mid B)\Pr(B)=\Pr(B\mid A)\Pr(A).$$

For three events:

$$\Pr(A\cap B\cap C)=\Pr(A)\,\Pr(B\mid A)\,\Pr(C\mid A\cap B).$$

This is how sequential experiments (draw without replacement, successive tests) are written as products.

三个事件的链式法则用于不放回抽取、序贯检验等。

---

## Step 3: Hand examples / 第三步：手算

**Die.** Fair die, $A=$“even”, $B=$“$>3$”.  
$A\cap B=\{4,6\}$, $\Pr(A\cap B)=1/3$, $\Pr(B)=1/2$ ⇒ $\Pr(A\mid B)=2/3$.

**Two fair coins — the classic trap.** Given at least one head, probability both are heads?  
Sample space $\{\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{HT},\mathrm{TH},\mathrm{TT}\}$. Conditioning event has 3 points; both heads is 1 of them → $\Pr=1/3$ (not $1/2$).  
Students who answer $1/2$ are treating the other coin as still “completely free” without restricting the joint space.

两枚公平硬币：至少一正的条件下两正的概率是 $1/3$，不是 $1/2$。

---

## Step 4: Independence preview / 第四步：独立预告

$A$ and $B$ are independent when $\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A)$ (equivalently $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$). Conditional probability then does not change the marginal — full treatment in [M1.6](M1.6.md).

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Conditioning **restricts** the sample space; recompute relative weights on what remains.  
2. $\Pr(A\mid B)$ and $\Pr(B\mid A)$ are different objects — swapping them is the Bayes/base-rate error.  
3. Multiplication rule turns sequential information into a product.  
4. “At least one head” is not the same as “a particular coin is a head”.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Swapping $\Pr(A\mid B)$ with $\Pr(B\mid A)$ | Read which event is given |
| Dividing by $\Pr(A)$ when condition was $B$ | Denominator = conditioning event |
| Conditioning on a null event | Definition requires $\Pr(B)>0$ |
| Answering $1/2$ for two-coin “both heads | at least one head” | Enumerate the three-point restricted space |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Two fair coins. Find $\Pr(\text{both heads}\mid\text{at least one head})$.

**Solution.**  
$\Pr(\text{both}\cap\text{at least one})=\Pr(\text{both})=1/4$.  
$\Pr(\text{at least one})=3/4$.  
Ratio $=1/3$.

## Practice / 练习

1. Fair die. $\Pr(\text{six}\mid\text{even})$?

2. Three events chain: write $\Pr(A\cap B\cap C)$ in two different parenthesizations.

3. Why is $\Pr(A\mid B)$ undefined when $\Pr(B)=0$?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $\{6\}$ among $\{2,4,6\}$ → $1/3$.

2. $\Pr(A)\Pr(B\mid A)\Pr(C\mid A\cap B)=\Pr(C)\Pr(B\mid C)\Pr(A\mid B\cap C)=\cdots$

3. Definition divides by $\Pr(B)$.

</details>
