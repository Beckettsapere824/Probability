# M7.3 Class-Size Paradox / 班级大小悖论

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M3.1](M3.1.md)

**Source anchors**: size-biased sampling; student-weighted vs class-weighted means.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Average class size **as experienced by a random student** is larger than the average of class sizes on the registrar’s list. Different sampling measures (size-biasing).

学生随机感受到的平均班额大于按班级列表的平均——抽样测度不同（长度偏倚）。

---

## Step 1: Two averages / 第一步：两种平均

Classes of sizes $n_1,\ldots,n_k$.  
Registrar: $\bar n=\frac{1}{k}\sum n_i$.  
Student-weighted: $\frac{\sum n_i^{2}}{\sum n_i}$.

---

## Step 2: Why larger / 第二步：为何更大

Variance identity: student mean $-$ registrar mean $\ge 0$, equality iff all sizes equal.

---

## Step 3: Hand check / 第三步：手算

Sizes 10, 30. Registrar mean $20$. Student-weighted $(100+900)/40=25$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Sampling proportional to size inflates the mean.  
2. Same data, different weights → different summaries.  
3. Paradox is resolved by stating who is sampled.  
4. Appears in networks (friendship paradox) and inspection paradox.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Claiming the two means must coincide | Only if sizes equal |
| Averaging without weights | State the measure |
| Using student mean as registrar KPI blindly | Mismatch of estimand |

---

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** Student-weighted mean class size is

A. always smaller than registrar mean  
B. at least the registrar mean  
C. always equal  
D. undefined

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Size bias.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Registrar mean for sizes $n_1,\ldots,n_k$ is

A. $\sum n_i^{2}/\sum n_i$  
B. $\frac{1}{k}\sum n_i$  
C. $\max n_i$  
D. $k$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Uniform over classes.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** Sizes 10 and 30. Student-weighted mean =

A. $20$  
B. $25$  
C. $30$  
D. $15$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$(100+900)/40=25$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** Equality of the two means holds when

A. always  
B. all class sizes equal  
C. $k=1$ only never  
D. variance infinite

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Zero variance of sizes.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** Size-biasing means sampling probability proportional to

A. $1/n_i$  
B. $n_i$  
C. $n_i^{2}$ only for mean  
D. uniform always

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Larger classes more students.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** Related “friendship paradox” says your friends have on average

A. fewer friends than you  
B. more friends than you (size bias on degrees)  
C. the same  
D. zero

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Degree bias.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** The gap equals (up to scaling) a

A. mean absolute deviation only  
B. variance term of the $n_i$  
C. median  
D. mode

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Variance identity.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** Resolving the paradox requires

A. deleting large classes  
B. stating which sampling measure is used  
C. forcing all $n_i=1$  
D. continuous time

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Clarify estimand.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
