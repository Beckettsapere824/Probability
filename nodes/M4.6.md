# M4.6 Poisson Distribution / 泊松分布

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M4.2 Binomial](M4.2.md), [M0.5 Series](M0.5.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Poisson limit; exam items on rare events, Poisson($\lambda$) with $\mathrm{Var}=\lambda$, sum of independent Poissons, conditional $X\mid X+Y=n$.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

You count rare events in a large number of opportunities: defects on a long cable, calls per minute, accidents per day, mutations on a genome. Each individual opportunity has tiny probability $p$, but there are $n$ of them with $np$ stable. The exact binomial $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$ becomes clumsy ($n$ huge, $p$ tiny); the **Poisson($\lambda$)** limit with $\lambda=np$ is the workable model.

在大量机会里数稀有事件：长导线瑕疵、每分钟来电、每日事故。单次机会概率 $p$ 极小，但 $n$ 很大且 $np$ 稳定。精确二项 $n$ 太大不好算；**Poisson($\lambda$)**（$\lambda=np$）是可用的极限模型。

---

## Step 1: PMF and the series check / 第一步：PMF 与级数验证

$$X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda), \qquad \Pr(X=k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}, \quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,\quad \lambda>0.$$

Why the $e^{-\lambda}$? Because

$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}=e^{\lambda}\implies\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}=1.$$

Without $e^{-\lambda}$ the masses do not sum to 1 — a common exam slip.

没有 $e^{-\lambda}$ 就不是概率，这是常见笔误。

---

## Step 2: Mean and variance both equal $\lambda$ / 第二步：均值方差都等于 $\lambda$

$$E(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}=\lambda e^{-\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}=\lambda.$$

(Shift index $j=k-1$.)

Similarly $E\bigl(X(X-1)\bigr)=\lambda^{2}$, so

$$\mathrm{Var}(X)=E(X^{2})-[E(X)]^{2}=\lambda^{2}+\lambda-\lambda^{2}=\lambda.$$

**Diagnostic:** if a count variable is claimed to be Poisson, mean and variance should be close. If sample variance is many times the sample mean, Poisson is a bad fit (overdispersion).

**识别：** 声称泊松的计数，均值应接近方差。样本方差远大于均值则过散，不宜硬套泊松。

---

## Step 3: Binomial limit (sketch you should be able to outline) / 第三步：二项极限

Fix $\lambda>0$, set $p_{n}=\lambda/n$. Then

$$\binom{n}{k}p_{n}^{k}(1-p_{n})^{n-k}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{k!}\Bigl(\frac{\lambda}{n}\Bigr)^{k}\Bigl(1-\frac{\lambda}{n}\Bigr)^{n-k}.$$

As $n\to\infty$:

- $\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{n^{k}}\to 1$,
- $\bigl(1-\lambda/n\bigr)^{n}\to e^{-\lambda}$,
- $\bigl(1-\lambda/n\bigr)^{-k}\to 1$.

Hence the mass tends to $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{k}/k!$.

**Exam use:** “$n=100$, $p=0.03$” → approximate by Poisson($\lambda=3$) when asked for a numerical probability that would be painful by binomial.

---

## Step 4: Independent sum and thinning / 第四步：独立和与稀疏

If $X\sim\mathrm{Po}(\lambda_{1})$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Po}(\lambda_{2})$ independent, then

$$X+Y\sim\mathrm{Po}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}).$$

(Proof via convolution or MGF $E(e^{tX})=\exp\bigl(\lambda(e^{t}-1)\bigr)$.)

**Conditional fact used in exams:** given $X+Y=n$, the conditional law of $X$ is $\mathrm{Binomial}\bigl(n,\lambda_{1}/(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\bigr)$. This is the discrete twin of “independent Poissons split by independent type”.

**考试用条件事实：** 给定 $X+Y=n$，$X\mid X+Y=n\sim\mathrm{Bin}\bigl(n,\tfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\bigr)$。

---

## Step 5: Link to exponential waiting / 第五步：与指数等待的联系

In a rate-$\lambda$ Poisson process, the number of events in a unit interval is $\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$, and the waiting time until the first event is $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$ ([M8.4](M8.4.md)). Counting and waiting are two sides of the same rate.

速率 $\lambda$ 的泊松过程：单位时间事件数 $\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$，等到第一次事件的时间 $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$。计数与等待是同一速率的两面。

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Poisson = limit of rare Bernoulli trials with fixed mean $\lambda$.  
2. Mean = variance = $\lambda$ is a fingerprint.  
3. Independent Poissons add their $\lambda$’s.  
4. Conditional on the sum, you get a binomial split proportional to the rates.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Dropping $e^{-\lambda}$ | Masses no longer sum to 1 |
| Using Poisson for strongly dependent events | Limit theorem needs near-independence |
| Confusing $\lambda$ with a success probability $p$ | $\lambda$ is a mean count, can exceed 1 |
| Computing $\Pr(X\ge 25)$ for $\lambda=15$ by hand term-by-term | Use software / tables / normal approx when allowed; exams often leave it as a sum |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Number of signals per hour is $\mathrm{Po}(15)$.  
(a) $\Pr(\text{no signal in an hour})$?  
(b) If $X,Y$ are i.i.d. $\mathrm{Po}(15)$ for two independent hours, find $\Pr(X+Y=0)$ and the law of $X+Y$.  
(c) Given $X+Y=10$, what is $\Pr(X=4)$?

**Solution.**

(a) $e^{-15}$.  
(b) $X+Y\sim\mathrm{Po}(30)$, $\Pr(X+Y=0)=e^{-30}$.  
(c) $X\mid X+Y=10\sim\mathrm{Bin}(10,1/2)$, so $\Pr(X=4\mid X+Y=10)=\binom{10}{4}(1/2)^{10}$.

## Practice / 练习

1. $X\sim\mathrm{Po}(2)$. Find $\Pr(X=0)$ and $\Pr(X\ge 1)$.

2. Outline why $\mathrm{Bin}(n,\lambda/n)\to\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$ termwise (main algebraic limits).

3. $X\sim\mathrm{Po}(3)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Po}(5)$ independent. Law of $X+Y$? $\Pr(X=2\mid X+Y=6)$?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $e^{-2}$, $1-e^{-2}$.

2. See Step 3: $\frac{n_{(k)}}{n^{k}}\to 1$, $(1-\lambda/n)^{n}\to e^{-\lambda}$.

3. $\mathrm{Po}(8)$; $\mathrm{Bin}(6,3/8)$ mass at 2: $\binom{6}{2}(3/8)^{2}(5/8)^{4}$.

</details>
