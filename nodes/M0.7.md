# M0.7 Absolute Values and Inequalities / 绝对值与不等式

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.1](M0.1.md)

**Source anchors**: triangle inequality; solving $|x-a|<c$; log inequalities for sample-size bounds.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Concentration bounds, confidence intervals, and “how large must $n$ be” questions all turn absolute values and inequalities into intervals or integer thresholds. We need fluent translation between $|X-\mu|\le c$ and interval statements, and careful handling of logarithms when the base is less than 1.

浓度不等式、置信区间、「$n$ 要多大」都会把绝对值与不等式变成区间或整数阈值。需要熟练在 $|X-\mu|\le c$ 与区间之间翻译，并在底数小于 1 时正确处理对数不等式方向。

---

## Step 1: Absolute value basics / 第一步：绝对值基础

$|x|\ge 0$ for all real $x$; $|x|=0\iff x=0$.

$|x-a|<c$ (with $c>0$) means $a-c<x<a+c$.

$|x-a|\ge c$ means $x\le a-c$ or $x\ge a+c$.

Triangle inequality: $|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$.

---

## Step 2: Probability language / 第二步：概率语言

$|X-\mu|\le 2\sigma$ is the event $X\in[\mu-2\sigma,\mu+2\sigma]$.

Markov / Chebyshev statements are already in absolute-value form; converting to intervals is required before computing numerical probabilities on a concrete distribution.

---

## Step 3: Log inequalities for $n$ / 第三步：对数求 $n$

Example: $(1-p)^{n}< \varepsilon$ with $0<p<1$, $0<\varepsilon<1$.

Take $\ln$: $n\ln(1-p)<\ln\varepsilon$.

Since $\ln(1-p)<0$, dividing reverses the inequality:

$$
n>\frac{\ln\varepsilon}{\ln(1-p)}.
$$

Always check the sign of the coefficient before dividing.

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

Solve $|x-2|<3$: $-1<x<5$.

Solve $|x-2|\ge 3$: $x\le -1$ or $x\ge 5$.

Smallest integer $n$ with $(0.9)^{n}\le 0.05$: $n\ge\ln 0.05/\ln 0.9\approx 28.43$, so $n=29$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Absolute-value inequalities split into two rays or one open interval.  
2. Triangle inequality is the metric backbone of many concentration proofs.  
3. When dividing an inequality by a negative number (including $\ln r$ for $0<r<1$), flip the inequality.  
4. Ceiling is required when $n$ must be an integer.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Writing $|x-a|<c$ as $x-a<c$ only | Must also have $-(x-a)<c$ |
| Not flipping inequality when dividing by $\ln(1-p)<0$ | Check sign every time |
| Using floor instead of ceil for minimal $n$ | Minimal integer $\ge$ real threshold uses ceiling |
| Confusing $|a+b|$ with $|a|+|b|$ equality cases | Equality holds when $a,b$ same sign (including zero) |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** $|x-2|<3$ is equivalent to

A. $x<5$  
B. $-1<x<5$  
C. $x<-1$ or $x>5$  
D. $x> -1$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$-3<x-2<3\Rightarrow -1<x<5$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Triangle inequality states

A. $|a+b|=|a|+|b|$  
B. $|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$  
C. $|a+b|\ge|a|+|b|$  
D. $|a-b|=|a|-|b|$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard triangle inequality is $\le$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** $|X-\mu|\le 2\sigma$ means $X$ lies in

A. $[\mu,\mu+2\sigma]$  
B. $[\mu-2\sigma,\mu+2\sigma]$  
C. $(-\infty,\mu-2\sigma]$  
D. $\{2\sigma\}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Closed interval centred at $\mu$ with radius $2\sigma$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** If $0<r<1$ and $r^{n}<\varepsilon$, then

A. $n<\ln\varepsilon/\ln r$  
B. $n>\ln\varepsilon/\ln r$  
C. $n=\varepsilon/r$  
D. $n<\varepsilon$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$\ln r<0$, so inequality flips: $n>\ln\varepsilon/\ln r$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** $|a|=0$ if and only if

A. $a>0$  
B. $a<0$  
C. $a=0$  
D. never

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**6.** Solve $|x|\ge 2$:

A. $-2\le x\le 2$  
B. $x\le -2$ or $x\ge 2$  
C. $x>2$  
D. $x<-2$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Outside the open interval $(-2,2)$, including endpoints.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** Smallest integer $n$ with $(1/2)^{n}\le 1/10$ is

A. 3  
B. 4  
C. 5  
D. 10

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$n\ge\log_{2}10\approx 3.32$, so $n=4$. Check: $1/16\le 1/10$, $1/8>1/10$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** $|a+b|=|a|+|b|$ holds when

A. always  
B. $a,b$ have opposite signs  
C. $a,b$ have the same sign (or one is zero)  
D. never

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Equality in triangle inequality when $a,b$ are nonnegative multiples of the same vector (here: same sign on $\mathbb{R}$).  
Answer: **C**.
</details>
