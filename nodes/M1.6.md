# M1.6 Independence of Events / 事件独立性

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.3 Conditional Probability](M1.3.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3; pairwise vs mutual; independence ≠ disjoint; product for sequences.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Sometimes learning that $B$ occurred does **not** change the probability of $A$. That modelling assumption — **independence** — must be stated explicitly. When it holds, joint probabilities factor into products.

有时知道 $B$ 发生了**并不会**改变 $A$ 的概率。这种建模假设——**独立性**——必须写明。成立时，交事件概率分解为乘积。

---

## Step 1: Definition (two events) / 第一步：两事件定义

Events $A,B$ are **independent** when

$$\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B).$$

If $\Pr(B)>0$, this is equivalent to $\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A)$.

Independence is about **probabilities**, not everyday “causation” — always check the numbers.

---

## Step 2: Mutual vs pairwise independence / 第二步：相互独立 vs 两两独立

A family is **mutually independent** when every subcollection factors. **Pairwise** only requires pairs. Pairwise does **not** imply mutual.

**Counter-example.** Two fair coins: $A=$ first heads, $B=$ second heads, $C=$ coins differ. Pairwise independent, but $\Pr(A\cap B\cap C)=0\neq\Pr(A)\Pr(B)\Pr(C)$.

---

## Step 3: Independence ≠ disjointness / 第三步：独立 ≠ 互斥

If $\Pr(A),\Pr(B)>0$ and $A\cap B=\emptyset$, then $\Pr(A\cap B)=0\neq\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$. Positive-probability events cannot be both independent and disjoint.

For independent $A,B$: $\Pr(A\cup B)=1-(1-\Pr(A))(1-\Pr(B))$.

---

## Step 4: Independence of complements / 第四步：补事件

If $A,B$ independent, so are $A$ and $B^{c}$, $A^{c}$ and $B$, $A^{c}$ and $B^{c}$.

---

## Step 5: Repeated independent trials / 第五步：重复独立试验

Specified sequence probability = product of trial probabilities (Bernoulli / binomial modelling assumption).

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Independence ⇔ joint = product ⇔ conditioning does not change the other event’s probability.  
2. Pairwise ≠ mutual; exams test this with three-event counter-examples.  
3. Independent and disjoint are almost opposites for positive-probability events.  
4. Independence of $A,B$ propagates to combinations of complements.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Confusing independence with mutual exclusivity | Product vs empty intersection |
| Checking only pairs when mutual independence is required | Verify all subcollections |
| Assuming independence without model justification | State it as an assumption |
| Forgetting complements inherit independence | Use Step 4 |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** Fair die. $A=$ even, $B=\{\,4,5,6\,\}$. Independent?

A. Yes, because $\Pr(A\cap B)=1/4$  
B. No, because $\Pr(A\cap B)=1/3\neq 1/4$  
C. Yes, because they overlap  
D. Cannot tell

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$\Pr(A)=1/2$, $\Pr(B)=1/2$, $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(\{4,6\})=1/3\neq 1/4$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Two positive-probability events that are mutually exclusive

A. must be independent  
B. cannot be independent  
C. must be exhaustive  
D. must have $\Pr=1/2$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Would require $\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=0$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** Pairwise independence of three events

A. always implies mutual independence  
B. does not imply mutual independence in general  
C. is impossible  
D. requires $\Pr=0$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Classic two-coin counter-example.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** Definition of independence of $A,B$:

A. $\Pr(A\cap B)=0$  
B. $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B)$  
C. $\Pr(A\cup B)=1$  
D. $A\subset B$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Product formula.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** If $A,B$ independent and $\Pr(A)=0.3$, $\Pr(B)=0.5$, then $\Pr(A\cup B)=$

A. $0.8$  
B. $0.65$  
C. $0.15$  
D. $0.5$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$0.3+0.5-0.15=0.65$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** If $A,B$ independent, then $A$ and $B^{c}$ are

A. exclusive  
B. independent  
C. exhaustive  
D. dependent always

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Complements inherit independence.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** Fair coins sequence HHTH under independence has probability

A. $1/2$  
B. $1/4$  
C. $1/8$  
D. $1/16$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$(1/2)^4=1/16$.  
Answer: **D**.
</details>

**8.** “If $A,B,C$ pairwise independent then mutually independent” is
