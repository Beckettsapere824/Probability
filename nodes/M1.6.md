# M1.6 Independence of Events / 事件独立性

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.3 Conditional Probability](M1.3.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3; pairwise vs mutual; independence ≠ disjoint; product for sequences; exam true/false on three events.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Sometimes learning that $B$ occurred does **not** change the probability of $A$. That modelling assumption — **independence** — must be stated explicitly. When it holds, joint probabilities factor into products, and repeated trials become easy to write.

有时知道 $B$ 发生了**并不会**改变 $A$ 的概率。这种建模假设——**独立性**——必须写明。成立时，交事件概率分解为乘积，重复试验也好写。

---

## Step 1: Definition (two events) / 第一步：两事件定义

Events $A,B$ are **independent** when

$$\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B).$$

If $\Pr(B)>0$, this is equivalent to $\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A)$ (and symmetrically $\Pr(B\mid A)=\Pr(B)$ when $\Pr(A)>0$).

Independence is a statement about **probabilities**, not about physical “causation”. Two events can be independent even when they feel related in everyday language, and dependent when they feel unrelated — always check the numbers.

若 $\Pr(B)>0$，等价于 $\Pr(A\mid B)=\Pr(A)$。独立是关于**概率**的陈述，不是日常因果语感；始终用数字检验。

---

## Step 2: Mutual vs pairwise independence / 第二步：相互独立 vs 两两独立

A finite family $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ is **mutually independent** when **every** subcollection factors:

$$\Pr\Bigl(\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j\Bigr)=\prod_{j\in J}\Pr(A_j)$$

for every nonempty $J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}$. In particular all pairs, all triples, … must factor.

**Pairwise independence** only requires the equality for pairs. Pairwise does **not** imply mutual.

**Standard counter-example (exam favourite).** Two fair coins. Let

- $A=$ first coin heads,  
- $B=$ second coin heads,  
- $C=$ the two coins differ.

Then $A,B,C$ are pairwise independent, but $\Pr(A\cap B\cap C)=0\neq\Pr(A)\Pr(B)\Pr(C)$.

有限族**相互独立**要求任意子族的交都等于各自概率之积。仅两两独立**不蕴含**相互独立。经典反例：两枚公平硬币上的 $A,B,C$。

---

## Step 3: Independence ≠ disjointness / 第三步：独立 ≠ 互斥

If $\Pr(A)>0$, $\Pr(B)>0$ and $A\cap B=\emptyset$, then

$$\Pr(A\cap B)=0\neq\Pr(A)\Pr(B).$$

Positive-probability events **cannot** be both independent and disjoint. Disjointness is about sets; independence is about product of probabilities.

For independent $A,B$:

$$\Pr(A\cup B)=\Pr(A)+\Pr(B)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=1-(1-\Pr(A))(1-\Pr(B)).$$

正概率事件不能既独立又互斥。独立时并的公式可化成 $1-(1-p_A)(1-p_B)$。

---

## Step 4: Independence of complements / 第四步：补事件

If $A,B$ independent, then so are $A$ and $B^{c}$, $A^{c}$ and $B$, $A^{c}$ and $B^{c}$.  
Proof sketch for $A$ and $B^{c}$:

$$\Pr(A\cap B^{c})=\Pr(A)-\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(A)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=\Pr(A)\Pr(B^{c}).$$

Useful when a problem asks for “neither” or “at least one fails”.

$A,B$ 独立 ⇒ 与各自补事件的各种组合仍独立。

---

## Step 5: Repeated independent trials / 第五步：重复独立试验

If trials are independent, the probability of a specified sequence is the product of the trial probabilities. Example: fair coins, sequence HHTH has probability $(1/2)^4$.  
This is the modelling assumption behind Bernoulli / binomial / geometric sequences ([M4.1](M4.1.md)–[M4.4](M4.4.md)).

独立试验：指定序列概率 = 各次概率之积。这是 Bernoulli/二项/几何序列的建模假设。

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Independence ⇔ joint = product of marginals ⇔ conditioning does not change the other event’s probability.  
2. Pairwise ≠ mutual; exams test the distinction with three-event counter-examples.  
3. Independent and disjoint are almost opposites for positive-probability events.  
4. Independence of $A,B$ propagates to all combinations of complements.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Confusing independence with mutual exclusivity | Product vs empty intersection |
| Checking only pairs when mutual independence is required | Verify all subcollections |
| Assuming independence without model justification | State it as an assumption |
| Forgetting that complements inherit independence | Use Step 4 |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Fair die. $A=$“even”, $B=$“$\ge 4$”. Are $A$ and $B$ independent?

**Solution.** $\Pr(A)=1/2$, $\Pr(B)=1/2$, $\Pr(A\cap B)=\Pr(\{4,6\})=1/3\neq 1/4$. Not independent.

**True/false style.** “If $A,B,C$ are pairwise independent, then they are mutually independent.” False — cite the two-coin counter-example.

---

## Practice / 练习

1. Fair die. $A=$ even, $B=$ $\ge 4$. Independent?

2. Show that if $A,B$ independent then $A$ and $B^{c}$ are independent.

3. Two fair coins. Define $A,B,C$ as in Step 2 and verify pairwise but not mutual independence.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. $1/3\neq 1/4$ → not independent.

2. $\Pr(A\cap B^{c})=\Pr(A)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=\Pr(A)(1-\Pr(B))$.

3. Each pair has intersection probability $1/4=\Pr\cdot\Pr$; triple intersection empty.

</details>
