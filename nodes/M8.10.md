# M8.10 Continuous Joint and Marginal / 连续联合与边缘

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M8.1 Continuous RV and pdf](M8.1.md), [M1.10 Joint discrete](M1.10.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch7 continuous joint; volume under surface; independence factorisation.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Two continuous measurements on the same experiment — height and weight, two waiting times, coordinates of a random point — need a **joint density**. Probability becomes volume under a surface; marginals are obtained by integrating out the other variable.

同一次试验上的两个连续量需要**联合密度**。概率变成曲面下的体积；边缘通过把另一变量积分消掉得到。

---

## Step 1: Joint density / 第一步：联合密度

A joint pdf $f_{X,Y}(x,y)$ satisfies

$$f_{X,Y}(x,y)\ge 0, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy=1.$$

For a region $A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$,

$$\Pr\bigl((X,Y)\in A\bigr)=\iint_{A}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy.$$

Point probabilities are still zero; probability lives in areas (volumes under the surface).

点概率仍为 0；概率在区域（曲面下体积）里。

---

## Step 2: Marginals / 第二步：边缘

$$f_X(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dy, \qquad f_Y(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx.$$

Integrate out the variable you do not need. The result is a valid one-dimensional pdf.

把不需要的变量积分掉，得到一维合法 pdf。

---

## Step 3: Independence / 第三步：独立

$X,Y$ independent iff

$$f_{X,Y}(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y) \quad\text{for (almost) all }x,y.$$

Then the joint surface is a product surface. Equivalent for CDFs: $F_{X,Y}(x,y)=F_X(x)F_Y(y)$.

独立 ⇔ 联合密度 = 边缘密度之积。

---

## Step 4: Hand example / 第四步：手算

$f(x,y)=1$ on the unit square $(0,1)\times(0,1)$ (and 0 elsewhere).  
This is already normalised ($\iint=1$).  
Marginal $f_X(x)=\int_0^1 1\,dy=1$ for $x\in(0,1)$ — so $X\sim U(0,1)$.  
Similarly $Y\sim U(0,1)$, and $f(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)$ ⇒ independent.

**Non-rectangular support.** If $f$ is constant on the triangle $0<x<y<1$, marginals are not uniform and $X,Y$ are dependent (support constraint couples them).

支撑是三角形时，边缘非均匀且 $X,Y$ 相依。

---

## Step 5: Expectation from the joint / 第五步：由联合求期望

$$E\bigl[g(X,Y)\bigr]=\iint g(x,y)f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy$$

(LOTUS in two dimensions). In particular $E(XY)$ for covariance needs this integral unless independence gives factorisation.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Joint continuous law = density on the plane; probability = volume.  
2. Marginal = integrate out the other coordinate.  
3. Independence ⇔ product of marginal densities.  
4. Support shape alone can force dependence (e.g. triangle).

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Treating $f(x,y)$ as a probability | It is density; integrate |
| Forgetting to check $\iint f=1$ | Normalise first |
| Claiming independence without checking product on the whole support | Support constraints matter |
| Using discrete sum notation for continuous joint | Double integral |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** $f(x,y)=1$ on the unit square. Find $f_X(0.3)$ and decide independence.

**Solution.** $f_X(0.3)=1$ (for $0.3\in(0,1)$). Product form holds ⇒ independent.

## Practice / 练习

1. Unit square joint as above. $\Pr(X<1/2,Y<1/2)$?

2. Why does a uniform density on $\{(x,y):0<x<y<1\}$ make $X$ and $Y$ dependent?

3. Write the marginal formula for $f_Y$.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. Area $1/4$.

2. The support is not a product set; large $X$ forces $Y$ even larger.

3. $f_Y(y)=\int f(x,y)\,dx$.

</details>
