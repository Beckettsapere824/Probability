# M1.1 Sample Space, Events, Probability Axioms / 样本空间、事件、概率公理

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.1 Sets and Event Language](M0.1.md)

**Source anchors**: SDST2601A Ch3 §3.1.3 Def 3.1 (Kolmogorov); classical model as special case.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

We want one mathematical language that works for dice, coins, continuous measurements, and infinite sequences of trials — without inventing a new rule each time.

我们要一套统一的数学语言，既能说骰子和硬币，也能说连续测量和无穷次试验——而不是每种情况另立规矩。

Kolmogorov’s answer (1933): fix a **sample space** of outcomes, call certain subsets **events**, and require a number $\Pr(E)$ on events to obey three axioms.

Kolmogorov（1933）：固定**样本空间**，把某些子集叫做**事件**，要求 $\Pr(E)$ 满足三条公理。

---

## Step 1: Sample space and events / 第一步：样本空间与事件

The **sample space** $\Omega$ is the set of all outcomes of the experiment you care about. An **event** is a subset $E\subseteq\Omega$ that you are allowed to assign probability to.

Examples:

- One die: $\Omega=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$.  
- Two ordered coin flips: $\Omega=\{\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{HT},\mathrm{TH},\mathrm{TT}\}$.  
- Continuous waiting time: $\Omega=(0,\infty)$.

**Choosing $\Omega$ is modelling.** Two coins listed as $\{\text{0 heads},\text{1 head},\text{2 heads}\}$ hide the fact that “1 head” arises in two ways; ordered pairs keep the symmetry honest.

**样本空间**是全部结果；**事件**是可赋概率的子集。选 $\Omega$ 本身就是建模——两次抛币用有序对，而不是「正面次数」。

---

## Step 2: Kolmogorov’s three axioms / 第二步：三条公理

A probability on the events of $\Omega$ assigns to each event $E$ a number $\Pr(E)$ such that:

1. **Non-negativity:** $\Pr(E)\ge 0$.  
2. **Normalization:** $\Pr(\Omega)=1$.  
3. **Countable additivity:** if $E_1,E_2,\ldots$ are pairwise disjoint, then
   $$\Pr\Bigl(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_i\Bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Pr(E_i).$$

**Immediate consequences:**

- $\Pr(\emptyset)=0$.  
- Finite additivity for disjoint unions.  
- $\Pr(E^{c})=1-\Pr(E)$.  
- Monotonicity: $A\subset B\implies\Pr(A)\le\Pr(B)$.

由此得空集概率 0、有限可加、补概率、单调性。

---

## Step 3: Classical (equally likely) model / 第三步：古典概型

When $\Omega$ is finite and every outcome is equally likely,

$$\Pr(A)=\frac{|A|}{|\Omega|}.$$

This satisfies the axioms **only after** you have chosen an $\Omega$ in which “equally likely” is honest. It is one model among others, not the definition of probability.

有限等可能时 $\Pr(A)=|A|/|\Omega|$。这是公理的一个特例，不是概率的定义。

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

**Disjoint union.** $\Omega=\{1,\ldots,6\}$, $A=\{1,3\}$, $B=\{2,4\}$.  
$\Pr(A\cup B)=4/6=\Pr(A)+\Pr(B)$.

**Two coins.** $\Omega$ four points each $1/4$. $A=$ at least one head → $\Pr(A)=3/4$; $B=$ two heads → $\Pr(B)=1/4$; $A\cap B=B$; $A\cup B=A$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Probability is a set function obeying three axioms — not “fraction of times” until a model is stated.  
2. Sample space choice is part of the model; bad $\Omega$ makes “equally likely” false.  
3. Countable additivity is the infinite version of “add probabilities of disjoint pieces”.  
4. Classical counting is a special case, not the foundation.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Any verbal description treated as equally likely $\Omega$ | Check symmetry of outcomes |
| $|A|/|\Omega|$ when outcomes are not equally likely | Use weighted models |
| Applying additivity to overlapping events | Only for disjoint unions (or inclusion-exclusion) |
| Forgetting $\Pr(\Omega)=1$ when normalising | Always check |

---

## Worked exam-style example / 考试风格例题

**Problem.** Fair coin tossed twice. List $\Omega$. $A=$ at least one head, $B=$ two heads. Find $\Pr(A)$, $\Pr(B)$, $\Pr(A\cap B)$, $\Pr(A\cup B)$.

**Solution.** $\Omega=\{\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{HT},\mathrm{TH},\mathrm{TT}\}$ each $1/4$. $\Pr(A)=3/4$, $\Pr(B)=1/4$, $\Pr(A\cap B)=1/4$, $\Pr(A\cup B)=3/4$.

## Practice / 练习

1. Why is $\{\text{0 heads},\text{1 head},\text{2 heads}\}$ a poor $\Omega$ for two fair coins if you want equal probability on atoms?

2. Derive $\Pr(E^{c})=1-\Pr(E)$ from the axioms.

3. Can $\Pr$ assign probability $1/2$ to every singleton in an infinite countable $\Omega$? Why or why not?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketches / 解答提示</summary>

1. “1 head” aggregates two outcomes; atoms are not equally likely under the honest product measure.

2. $\Omega=E\cup E^{c}$ disjoint ⇒ $1=\Pr(E)+\Pr(E^{c})$.

3. No: countable additivity would give total probability $\infty$.

</details>
