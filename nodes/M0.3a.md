# M0.3a Binomial Coefficient Boundaries / 组合数边界约定

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.3 Factorials, Permutations, Combinations](M0.3.md)

**Source anchors**: convention $\binom{n}{k}=0$ outside $0\le k\le n$; keeps PMF and sum limits clean.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Formulas involving $\binom{n}{k}$ often appear under sums or in definitions of probability mass functions. Declaring the binomial coefficient to be zero when the lower index is negative or exceeds $n$ lets us write a single expression without awkward case distinctions.

涉及 $\binom{n}{k}$ 的求和或 PMF 定义常会碰到非法下标。约定越界时组合数为 0，可让公式统一、无需额外分情况。

---

## Step 1: The zero convention / 第一步：零约定

For integers $n\ge 0$ and any integer $k$,

$$
\binom{n}{k}=0\quad\text{whenever }k<0\text{ or }k>n.
$$

Together with the already-known values

$$
\binom{n}{0}=\binom{n}{n}=1,
$$

the binomial coefficient is now defined for every integer $k$.

---

## Step 2: Why it is useful / 第二步：为何有用

- In a binomial PMF the sum $\sum_k\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ automatically runs only over the legal $k$ if we write the sum over all integers.
- Inclusion-exclusion or hockey-stick identities stay valid without extra indicator functions.
- “Impossible selections” (choose 5 items from 3) evaluate to zero instead of being undefined.

---

## Step 3: Hand checks / 第三步：手算

- $\binom{3}{5}=0$ (cannot choose 5 from 3)  
- $\binom{3}{0}=1$  
- $\binom{3}{-1}=0$  
- $\binom{5}{5}=1$

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Illegal lower indices yield zero, not an error.  
2. The convention is compatible with Pascal’s identity and generating functions.  
3. Always state the range of summation explicitly when teaching, even if the formula is “self-limiting”.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Claiming $\binom{n}{k}$ is undefined for $k>n$ | Use the zero convention |
| Writing a sum from $k=0$ to $\infty$ without justifying the zero terms | Note that terms vanish for $k>n$ |
| Forgetting $\binom{n}{0}=1$ | Memorise the two boundary ones |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** $\binom{3}{5}$ equals

A. undefined  
B. 0  
C. 1  
D. 3

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$k=5>3=n$, so by the zero convention the value is 0.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** $\binom{4}{0}$ equals

A. 0  
B. 1  
C. 4  
D. undefined

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Standard boundary value $\binom{n}{0}=1$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** $\binom{5}{-2}$ equals

A. $-10$  
B. 0  
C. 1  
D. undefined

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Negative lower index → 0 by convention.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**4.** In the binomial theorem expansion of $(1+x)^n$, the coefficient of $x^{n+1}$ is

A. $\binom{n}{n+1}=0$  
B. $\binom{n}{n+1}=1$  
C. undefined, so the term is omitted by hand  
D. $n+1$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

The zero convention makes the coefficient automatically zero; the term does not appear.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**5.** Which statement is correct under the standard convention?

A. $\binom{n}{k}$ is defined only for $0\le k\le n$  
B. $\binom{n}{k}=0$ for $k<0$ or $k>n$  
C. $\binom{n}{k}$ can be negative  
D. $\binom{n}{n+1}=n$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

The zero convention extends the definition to all integers $k$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** $\binom{7}{7}$ equals

A. 0  
B. 1  
C. 7  
D. $7!$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$\binom{n}{n}=1$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** When writing $\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}a_k$, the sum is effectively finite because

A. the series diverges  
B. terms with $k\notin\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$ are zero  
C. $n$ is infinite  
D. we never sum over negative $k$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Zero convention kills every illegal index.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
