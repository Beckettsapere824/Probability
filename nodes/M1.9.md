# M1.9 Classical Equiprobable Model / 古典概型

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M1.1](M1.1.md), [M0.3](M0.3.md)

**Source anchors**: $\Pr(A)=|A|/|\Omega|$; ordered vs unordered sample spaces; consistent counting.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

When every atomic outcome is equally likely, probability reduces to counting. The hard part is choosing $\Omega$ so that “equally likely” is honest, and keeping the same counting convention in numerator and denominator.

原子等可能时，概率化为计数。难点是选出让「等可能」成立的 $\Omega$，并保持分子分母同一口径。

---

## Step 1: Formula / 第一步：公式

Finite $\Omega$, uniform on atoms:

$$
\Pr(A)=\frac{|A|}{|\Omega|}.
$$

This is a **model**, not a definition of probability. It satisfies Kolmogorov’s axioms under uniformity.

---

## Step 2: Ordered vs unordered / 第二步：有序与无序

Two fair coins:

- Ordered: $\Omega=\{\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{HT},\mathrm{TH},\mathrm{TT}\}$, $|\Omega|=4$, $\Pr(\text{two heads})=1/4$.  
- Unordered “number of heads”: $\{0,1,2\}$ is **not** uniform — “1 head” hides two ordered outcomes.

Drawing two cards without replacement: decide whether order matters; never mix $P(n,k)$ in the denominator with $C(n,k)$ in the numerator.

---

## Step 3: Consistency rule / 第三步：口径一致

Whatever scheme defines $|\Omega|$ must define $|A|$.  
If outcomes are ordered pairs, favourable outcomes are ordered pairs.  
If combinations, both sides use combinations.

---

## Step 4: Hand checks / 第四步：手算

Fair die: $\Pr(\text{even})=3/6=1/2$.

Two fair dice, ordered pairs: $\Pr(\text{sum}=7)=6/36=1/6$.

Three fair coins, at least one head: $1-(1/8)=7/8$, or count $7$ of $8$ ordered triples.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. Classical probability = counting under a uniform model.  
2. Sample-space design is modelling; bad $\Omega$ breaks uniformity.  
3. Numerator and denominator must use the same type of object.  
4. “At least one” often easier via complement even in classical settings.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using $\{0,1,2\}$ heads as uniform for two coins | Use ordered 4-point space |
| Mixing permutations and combinations | Fix one convention |
| Assuming any verbal list is equiprobable | Check physical symmetry |
| Forgetting order in “first ace, then king” style problems | Explicit ordered $\Omega$ |

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** Two fair coins, $\Omega=\{\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{HT},\mathrm{TH},\mathrm{TT}\}$. $\Pr(\text{two heads})=$

A. $1/3$  
B. $1/4$  
C. $1/2$  
D. $2/3$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

One favourable of four equally likely.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**2.** Why is $\Omega=\{\text{0 heads},\text{1 head},\text{2 heads}\}$ a poor uniform model for two fair coins?

A. It is infinite  
B. The atom “1 head” is not equally likely with the others under the product measure  
C. Coins are continuous  
D. It has too many points

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

“1 head” aggregates HT and TH.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** Fair die. $\Pr(\text{prime face})$ with primes $\{2,3,5\}$:

A. $1/2$  
B. $1/3$  
C. $1/6$  
D. $2/3$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$3/6=1/2$.  
Answer: **A**.
</details>

**4.** Two fair dice (ordered). Number of outcomes with sum $7$:

A. $5$  
B. $6$  
C. $7$  
D. $36$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**5.** Classical formula $\Pr(A)=|A|/|\Omega|$ requires

A. independence  
B. finite $\Omega$ with equally likely atoms  
C. continuous $\Omega$  
D. $\Pr(A)=0$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Uniform finite model.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** Drawing 2 cards from 52 without replacement, order ignored. $|\Omega|=$

A. $52\times 51$  
B. $\binom{52}{2}$  
C. $52$  
D. $2^{52}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Combinations.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**7.** Three fair coins. $\Pr(\text{at least one head})$ via complement:

A. $1/8$  
B. $7/8$  
C. $1/2$  
D. $3/8$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$1-\Pr(\text{TTT})=1-1/8=7/8$.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**8.** Mixing $P(n,k)$ in the denominator with $C(n,k)$ in the numerator is

A. always correct  
B. a consistency error  
C. required for without-replacement  
D. only for dice

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Same object type on both sides.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
