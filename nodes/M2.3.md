# M2.3 Without Replacement, Ordered / 无放回有序抽样

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M2.1](M2.1.md)

**Source anchors**: $P(n,k)=n!/(n-k)!$; rankings; dealing ordered cards.

---

## What problem are we solving? / 我们要解决什么问题？

Sequences of length $k$ from $n$ distinct items **without** repeats: rankings, ordered draws without replacement, first–second–third place.

从 $n$ 个不同对象中取长度 $k$、**不允许重复**的序列：排名、无放回有序抽取。

---

## Step 1: Count / 第一步：计数

$$P(n,k)=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1).$$

First position $n$ choices, second $n-1$, …, $k$-th $n-k+1$.

---

## Step 2: Relation to combinations / 第二步：与组合的关系

$$\binom{n}{k}=\frac{P(n,k)}{k!}.$$

Unordered hand = ordered sequences of the same $k$ items divided by $k!$ orders.

---

## Step 3: Hand checks / 第三步：手算

$P(6,2)=6\cdot5=30$.  
$P(5,3)=5\cdot4\cdot3=60$.  
$P(n,n)=n!$, $P(n,1)=n$, $P(n,0)=1$.

---

## Must-build intuitions / 必须建立的直觉

1. No replacement + order → falling factorial $P(n,k)$.  
2. Divide by $k!$ to pass to unordered.  
3. $P(n,k)=0$ formally when $k>n$ (empty product convention aside).  
4. Contrast $n^{k}$ when repeats allowed.

---

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

| Pitfall | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Using $n^{k}$ when items cannot repeat | Use $P(n,k)$ |
| Using $\binom{n}{k}$ when order is recorded | Use $P$ then maybe divide |
| $P(n,k)$ for $k>n$ | Zero (impossible) |

---

## Practice / 练习（选择题）

**1.** $P(6,2)=$

A. $12$  
B. $15$  
C. $30$  
D. $36$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

$6\cdot5=30$.  
Answer: **C**.
</details>

**2.** $P(n,k)$ counts

A. unordered without replacement  
B. ordered without replacement  
C. ordered with replacement  
D. multisets

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Definition.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

**3.** $\binom{n}{k}$ relates to $P(n,k)$ by

A. $\binom{n}{k}=P(n,k)\cdot k!$  
B. $\binom{n}{k}=P(n,k)/k!$  
C. $\binom{n}{k}=P(n,k)$  
D. $\binom{n}{k}=n^{k}$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Divide out the $k!$ orders.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>

Answer: **B**.
</details>

**6.** Ranking 3 people out of 10 for 1st–2nd–3rd uses

**7.** $P(n,1)=$

A. $0$

<details>

</details>

**8.** When $k>n$, $P(n,k)$ is

A. $n^{k}$  
B. $0$ (impossible)  
C. $\binom{n}{k}$  
D. $k!$

<details>
<summary>详细过程 / Solution</summary>

Cannot choose more distinct items than available.  
Answer: **B**.
</details>
